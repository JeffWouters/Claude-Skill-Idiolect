The bench spent the winter where it always does, under the crab apple at the bottom of the garden, and by March it looked tired. The green paint had lifted along the slats in small blisters. Where water had collected in the joints, the wood underneath had gone grey and soft at the edges. Moss was working its way into the grain of the armrests. None of it was serious yet, but another wet season would make it serious, so on the first dry weekend in April I carried it onto the patio and got to work.

Sanding is the unglamorous part, and most of the job. I started with a coarse eighty-grit paper to take off the flaking paint and the worst of the weathering, then moved to one-twenty to smooth what was left. The flat slats were easy. The curved arms and the gaps between the backrest spindles took far longer, and I finished those by hand with a folded sheet, working the paper into corners the sander couldn't reach. By the end the bench was a patchwork of bare pale wood and old green, with fine dust over everything, me included. I wiped it down with a damp cloth and left it to dry fully overnight, because paint over damp timber is a waste of an afternoon.

Choosing the paint took longer than it should have. I'd assumed I would match the old green, but standing in the shop I found myself holding a tin of deep slate blue. In the end I settled on a microporous exterior wood paint, which lets moisture escape from the timber instead of trapping it and blistering again. The colour mattered less than that.

The first coat went on thin and looked disappointing: streaky, with the old green showing through at the edges. That's what first coats do. I let it cure for a full day, gave it a light rub with fine paper to knock back any raised grain, and then applied the second coat slowly, one slat at a time, following the grain.

The second coat is where the bench came back. The colour deepened and evened out, and the brush marks settled flat. By evening it looked newer than it had in years, and I didn't sit on it for another two days.
