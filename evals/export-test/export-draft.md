The bench spent the winter by the back fence. It should not have. I meant to move it into the shed in October. I say that every year. By March the paint had lifted along every slat. Bare grey wood showed through in 20 places. I counted.

First I let it dry. That took 2 weeks of the kind of weather that looks dry and is not. I waited until the wood felt warm under my hand. Not cool. Warm.

Then the sanding. I started with 80 grit on a cork block. The loose paint came off in long curls. The sound paint I left alone. There is no point chasing every old coat back to bare wood. Then 120 grit over everything. The arms took longest. They are rounded and they catch the rain first. So they were the worst.

Choosing the paint took longer than the sanding. Here is the count. 3 shops. 11 tins picked up and put down. 1 tin bought. I wanted the same green as before. Nobody makes it now. So I bought a darker one. It said exterior wood on the side and 2 coats on the back. I believed the side. I did not quite believe the back.

The first coat went on thin. That is what the tin said to do. It looked patchy. It always looks patchy. I left it 24 hours and tried not to look at it.

The second coat is the one that matters. It is also the one I rush. I did not rush it this time. I loaded the brush with less paint than you think you need. I worked along the grain. One slat at a time, end to end. Underneath the seat first. That way I would not kneel in wet paint later. I have done that before. Twice.

And then I left it alone. 2 days. No cups on the arms and no cat on the seat.

This morning I sat on it with a cup of tea. The paint was hard. The green is darker than the old one. It looks fine. The empty tin is still on the back step with its lid on.
