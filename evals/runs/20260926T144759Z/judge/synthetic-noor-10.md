## The passage (by the author)

And then the weft. This is the part I like. I go across, side to side, over one line and under the next. Over, under, over, under. At the end of each row I turn and come back. Under where I went over. Over where I went under.

Slowly a small square of cloth grows over the hole. A woven patch. Firm and flat.

The rule is to keep it loose. Not slack. Loose. If you pull tight, the patch puckers. Then it rubs a blister on your heel. I learned that on darn number two.

A patch the size of a coin takes me 30 minutes. A big heel hole takes an hour. I do it in the evening, in the chair by the lamp. The radio on. A cup of tea going cold on the arm.

Some people buy new socks. I know. Socks are cheap. A new pair costs less than an hour of my time, if you count it that way.

But I do not count it that way. The hour is not work. It is a quiet hour with my hands busy. And the sock is better after. A darned heel is thicker than the old heel. It lasts longer than the wool around it. So the next hole is always somewhere new.

My grandfather darned his own socks. He was a fisherman. He could darn in the dark by feel. He showed me once, when I was small. I did not pay much attention. Years later I found his mushroom in a box. Dark wood, worn smooth. I tried to use it. I could not remember how. So I taught myself from a book. The bulb works better than the mushroom for me. I kept the mushroom anyway.

These grey socks will not last for ever. The wool at the ankle is getting thin now, all the way round. That is hard to darn. When that goes, they go.

## Draft A

Once the vertical threads are in place across the patch, the second half of the darn begins: weaving the crosswise threads through them. This is where the repair turns from a set of loose strands into something that looks and behaves like fabric.

Work from one side of the patch to the other, taking the needle over one vertical thread and under the next, then reversing the pattern on the way back. Each pass locks the previous one in place. Anchor the ends in the sound wool beyond the outline, just as you did with the first layer.

The one thing to watch is tension. It is tempting to pull each thread snug, because tight feels secure. It is not. If you pull the crosswise threads tight, the patch gathers and bunches, and you end up with a hard ridge inside the sock that you will feel with every step. Let the threads lie flat and relaxed against the rounded support. The weave holds itself together; it does not need to be strained to be strong.

It is slow work, and that is part of its appeal. An evening spent darning feels restful in a way that few other tasks do. Your hands are busy, your attention is gentle, and there is a finished object at the end of it. And the result is not a lesser version of the sock. A well-darned heel is thicker and stronger than the original knitting, and in my experience the reinforced heels outlast the rest of the sock.

## Draft C

The vertical threads are in. Now the cross threads. This is the weaving part, and it is where most darns go wrong.

Start at one corner of the patch. Take the needle over the first vertical thread, under the next, over the next. All the way across. Take a small stitch into the good wool at the edge. Turn. Come back the other way, under where you went over, over where you went under. Then again. Row after row, until the patch is filled in.

Do not pull tight. That is the one rule. It is tempting. Each row looks neater if you give it a tug. But the tension adds up. Pull every row a little and by the end the patch has drawn in on itself. It bunches. It puckers the sock around it. Then it rubs when you walk, which is how you got the hole in the first place. Leave each row lying flat and a little loose. Push it up against the last one with the tip of the needle. Let the weave hold it, not the pull.

When it is done, it looks like a small square of cloth set into the sock. It is. You made it.

I find the time goes quietly. A sock, a bulb, a needle, an evening. No screen. Just the next row, and the next. It is restful in a way not much else is.

And it works. A heel I have darned lasts longer than the heel the sock came with. I have socks where the darn is the strongest part. The wool around it wears out first.
