I darn my socks. Not all of them. The wool ones. The old ones worth the time.

You do not need much. Here is the list. A long needle with a big eye. Yarn that matches the sock or near enough. And something round to hold the sock in shape. A darning mushroom if you have one. An old light bulb if you do not. The bulb works just as well. It is smooth and the right size for a heel.

First look at the hole. Then look past it. A hole in a sock does not come from nowhere. The wool around it has been wearing thin for a while. Hold the sock up to the light and you will see it. A thin pale patch much bigger than the hole itself. That is the part to mend. Not just the gap.

If you close only the hole, the thin wool next to it goes next. Right beside the neat new patch. [real failed darn needed]. So I mend the whole weak area. It takes longer. It lasts.

So the sock goes over the bulb with the hole on top. Pull it snug so the knit lies flat. Not stretched. Just flat.

Then mark out the patch. I run a line of small stitches around the whole weak area. A box, roughly. It should sit well into the good wool on every side. That line is the edge of the darn. Everything happens inside it.

Then the first layer. Start at one side of the box and lay a thread straight up across it. Then back down, next to the first. Then up again. Rows of parallel threads close together. From one side of the box to the other. Where there is still wool, you stitch through it. Where there is a hole, the thread just spans the gap.

That is half the job. The other half goes across.

Tonight the sock is on the bulb. One layer done.
