The old seal came out in pieces. It was flat, hard and cracked along one edge. No wonder the tap dripped.

So I took it to the local hardware store. I put it on the counter and asked for the same again. It cost [price needed]. That is the whole fix, really. One small ring of rubber.

Here is the rest of it.

First I cleaned the seat where the seal sits. Grit and scale build up there. A new seal on a dirty seat will leak again. Then I pressed the new seal in with my thumb. Not with a screwdriver. A screwdriver can nick the rubber, and a nick is a leak.

Then the valve went back in. I turned it by hand until it stopped. Then a little more with the spanner. Not hard. Brass is soft and the threads strip easily. [example needed: a past slip with a thread]. I said I would be more careful. So I was.

Then the handle. It only fits one way. I put it back the way it came off.

So then the water. This is the part people rush. I did not. I opened the supply valve a quarter turn and waited. The pipes knocked, then spat. Air came out of the tap first. Then a thin run of water. Then a full one. Another quarter turn. Then another. If you open it all at once, the full force hits the new seal in one go. That is how you undo an hour of work in a second.

The fix itself took [time needed]. Getting ready took far longer. Finding the tools. Clearing out the cupboard under the sink. Working out which valve was which, and which way it turned. That is always the way.

The spare seals went in a small bag. The bag went in the drawer with the spanner. Next time they will be there.

This morning the tap is dry. I checked it twice.
