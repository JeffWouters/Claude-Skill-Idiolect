There is a school of persons--they are usually proud of belonging to it--who will not tell the small lies of courtesy. They will not say they are delighted when they are merely present, nor that the soup was excellent when it was only soup. They call this honesty. I call it, on the whole, bad manners with a good conscience. The little social falsehood is not told for the liar's benefit; it is told to spare some one else a pang, and it deceives nobody who knows the language. When I tell my hostess that I have had a charming evening, she does not take it for sworn testimony, and I do not offer it as such. It is a façon de parler, as much as "yours faithfully" at the foot of a letter to a stranger.

Some one will object that truth is truth, and that a lie is a lie whatever its size. Granted; but the blunt truth-teller is seldom so disinterested as he supposes. His candour is a luxury, and like most luxuries it is paid for by other people. He enjoys the sensation of his own sincerity; his friends endure it. I fancy that few of them endure it for long. [example needed: a friendship lost to needless candour] One can say a hard thing when a hard thing is needed--and it sometimes is--but to say it on every trivial occasion is not virtue. It is self-indulgence.

The truth-teller has a second argument, which he thinks unanswerable. His praise, he says, is worth having precisely because it is rare. Well: is it? Praise that comes once in a long while is not treasured; it is simply forgotten, or disbelieved when it arrives. And the habitual critic does not, in practice, save up his praise for the great occasion. He loses the habit of it. He has so trained his eye to find the flaw that he can no longer see the thing itself (and he calls this discernment!), and when at last something deserves his admiration, he has nothing left to give it but a qualification. The point is that generosity, like any other faculty, withers if it is never used. Let us keep our small lies, then, and keep them kindly. They are the small change of affection. The man who won't spend it ends with none.
