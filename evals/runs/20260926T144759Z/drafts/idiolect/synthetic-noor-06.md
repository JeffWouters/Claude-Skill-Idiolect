The kitchen tap drips. It has dripped for a while. I told myself it was nothing important.

So I measured it. I caught the drips overnight and measured them in the morning. [amount needed]. That is not nothing. That is real water wasted, down the drain, every night and every day.

So the tap is getting fixed. It is an old brass one, heavy and a bit stiff. Here is how I started.

First the drain. I put the plug in and laid a cloth over it. Small parts roll. A screw down the drain would end the job before it began. Not a risk worth taking.

Then the water. The supply valve is under the sink, at the back. It has not been turned in years and it did not want to move. I did not force it. I worked it a little one way, then a little the other, until it gave. Then I turned it off fully and opened the tap to be sure. A last trickle. Then nothing.

Then the handle. There is a cap on top, and a screw under the cap. The cap prised off with a thin blade. The screw came out. The handle lifted off.

Then the valve itself. This is the part to do carefully. The valve sits tight in the body of the tap. When you turn it with a spanner, the whole tap wants to turn with it. And if the tap turns, it can twist the pipes under the sink. So I held the body of the tap with one hand and turned the valve with the other. Firm. Not sudden. [example needed, how I braced it].

Eventually it loosened. Then it wound out by hand.

Tonight the valve sits on the cloth by the sink. The old seal is still on it.
