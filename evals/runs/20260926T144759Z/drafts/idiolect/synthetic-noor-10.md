The first layer of the darn is done. Rows of thread run up and down across the weak patch. Now the second layer.

So the needle goes across. Side to side. Over one thread, under the next. Then back the other way, under where you went over. That is the weave. It turns a set of loose threads into cloth.

Here is the one rule. Do not pull tight. It is tempting. A tight thread feels firm and neat. But pull each row snug and it draws the sides in. Row by row, the whole darn puckers. Then it bunches into a hard ridge, and a ridge under your heel is worse than a hole. So leave each thread lying flat. Not slack. Just flat. Then push the rows close with the tip of the needle.
