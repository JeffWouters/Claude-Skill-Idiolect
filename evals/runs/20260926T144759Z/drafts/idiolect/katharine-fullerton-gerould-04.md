We have all heard the phrase "plain living and high thinking," and most of us have heard it used as if the one produced the other. I have never been able to see why. That a man should black his own boots is entirely his affair; there is nothing disgraceful in it, and a good deal that is sensible. But it is not more civilized than having them blacked. The hand that wields the brush is not thereby ennobled, and the mind above it is not thereby set free. On the contrary, the mind is, for the moment, pre-occupied with boots. A woman may spend a whole morning over the grate and emerge, perhaps, with a cleaner grate, but she will hardly come away with a loftier view of the universe than she took into it; and I do not see why any one should expect her to. Frugality may be a necessity or a discipline; it is not, of itself, a school of thought.

Consider what the graceful traditions of living actually require. A well-kept house, a table properly laid, a garden that is something more than a patch of cabbages--these do not maintain themselves. They need people who know how to keep them, and who have given years to learning it. Some of those people are the owners; many of them, to be sure, are servants. The butler who knows how the thing ought to be done is as much a custodian of civilization as the scholar who knows how a Greek verb ought to be conjugated--rather more, perhaps, since fewer people will miss the verb. Do away with him in the name of simplicity, and you do not simplify the tradition. You end it.

Some one will say that the tradition is not worth keeping if it costs another man his independence. I do not think the case is so simple, but let it pass. My point is only this: when every one does everything, no one does anything well. The levelling-down that is so much admired produces a nation of amateurs--amateur cooks, amateur gardeners, amateur carpenters, and, in the end, amateur thinkers, since the thinking must be done in whatever hour is left over from the scrubbing. It isn't high thinking that follows plain living. It is tired thinking. That is a very different thing.
