I take the same train every day. It is not an exciting trip. That is the point.

I used to work on the train. Laptop open. Emails half read before the first stop. Then I gave it up. Not for a big reason. I just noticed I got nothing done and saw nothing either. So now the laptop stays in the bag.

What I got back was time that is mine. Nobody can reach me in the carriage. Or they can. But nobody expects me to answer. It is free time in the plain sense. It costs nothing and nobody else wants it.

Here is how I know where I am without looking up at the signs.

Most days. When it is there, I look for it. When it is not, I look anyway. Then [landmark needed] before the next stop. And so on, all the way in.

The same view day after day is not dull. It is the opposite. Once you know a route well, you see what has changed. A new thing stands out because nothing else does.

So I see the seasons turn on the same few metres of bank. Green, then brown, then bare. I see frost on the fields on the first cold morning, white in the shade. Gone in the sun. I see fresh paint on a wall by the line. A new piece of street art. Bright and sharp, where last week there was an old one. And for a while there was a young musician on the platform at [station needed].

None of that is news. None of it would ever make it into my work. But I would have missed all of it with the laptop open.

This morning the bird was there. Frost on the bank. I watched until the next stop.
