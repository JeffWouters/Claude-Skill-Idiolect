I bled the radiators today. It is a small job. It is also one that makes the whole house better.

Here is the order I do it in.

First the heating goes off, so the water is still. Then a radiator key, and a cloth. The key fits the square valve at the top corner of each radiator. I hold the cloth under it and turn the key slowly. Not far. A quarter turn is plenty.

Then you wait. The air comes out first with a hiss. It can take longer than you think. Then a spit of water, then a steady bead.

That is the moment to stop. Close the valve gently. Not hard. The valve is small and made of soft brass. If you force it, you can strip it or crack the seat. Then you have a leak where you had a cold spot. So I turn it until it stops, and no further.

Then the next radiator. And the next.

After that, the boiler. Every drop that came out of the valves came out of the system. So the pressure has dropped. The gauge on the front of the boiler shows it. Mine was down to [reading needed] bar.

So I refill it through the filling loop. That is the short pipe under the boiler with a tap at each end. Open one, then the other. Water runs in from the mains. Watch the needle. When it reaches about 1.2 bar, close both taps. Not more. That is the level I aim for.

Then the heating goes back on.

The difference shows within the hour. The heat spreads evenly now. Top to bottom, end to end. No cold band along the top of each radiator.

It will not last. Air gets back into the system over the year. It always does. So next autumn I will do it all again.

Tonight the radiators are warm all over. The key is back in the drawer.
