Nobody who has not stood behind the counter of a bookshop for a whole day can quite appreciate the extraordinary variety of what is asked of the person who stands there.  The public imagines, I believe, that a bookseller sells books.  He does.  But he also advises, consoles, instructs, arbitrates and, upon occasion, guesses, and he is expected to do all of these things promptly, cheerfully and without the least appearance of surprise.

Consider, to begin with, the customer who is looking for a present.  This customer is the most hopeful of all at the door and the most despondent of all at the counter.  He wants something for somebody.  He does not know what.  He knows only what he does not want, and he discovers this by the simple method of rejecting everything you show him.  You offer him a novel, and it is too light.  You offer him an essayist, and it is too heavy.  You offer him poetry, and he looks at you as though you had suggested something slightly improper.  Well, you proceed through the entire stock in this fashion, and at the end of it he thanks you very kindly and says that he will think it over.

Then there is the constant change of altitude, so to speak.  At one moment you are called upon to discuss, with a learned person, some nice point of scholarship [example needed: a scholarly query], and you must do it gravely and with every appearance of erudition.  At the next you are required to find, for somebody else entirely, some trifle of no consequence whatever [example needed: a trivial query].  There is no interval between these transactions.  You descend from Olympus to the parlour in the time it takes to turn round, and you are not permitted to look dizzy.

And the demands themselves!  In a single day you may be asked for a book on the care of babies, for something to read on a journey, not too long, not too short, not too exciting, and for [example needed: further customer requests].  You supply what you can.  You apologise for what you cannot.  And you go home in the evening feeling, in a modest way, like an encyclopaedia that has been rather roughly handled.
