There is a sheet of paper on our fridge. It has been there for years. Not the same sheet, of course. The same idea.

It is a to-do list for the house. Nothing more clever than that. It sits on the fridge door where everyone can see it. A pen stays next to it.

Here is how it works.

The sheet has three parts. At the top, groceries. In the middle, quick jobs. Things that take no time and never get done. [quick job needed]. At the bottom, the big jobs. The ones that need a weekend or a plan or a trip to the shop.

Then there is the one rule. Whoever uses up something writes it down. Last of the milk. Last of the rice. You write it on the sheet before the empty box goes in the bin. Not later. Later means never. So the list fills up by itself. Nobody has to check the cupboards before a shop.

I did not start with paper. I tried the other ways first. Phone apps, [number needed] of them. They worked for me and nobody else. Nobody opens an app to note that the salt ran out. Then a notebook. It lived in a drawer. A list in a drawer is a list nobody reads. Then a whiteboard. [whiteboard failure needed]. It went the same way as the rest.

So, paper. Paper on the fridge wins because you cannot miss it. You open the fridge all day long. Every time, the list is right there in front of you. It does not need charging. It does not need a login. It just needs a pen that works.

That is all it is. It is not a system. It is a habit on a fridge door.
