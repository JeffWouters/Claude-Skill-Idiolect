Every autumn the radiators start to knock. Then they gurgle. So every autumn I bleed them.

It is not a hard job. It is just one I only do once a year, so I forget the order. Here it is, written down.

First the keys. I have [number needed] radiator keys. That sounds like a lot. It is not. They go missing. [where they turn up needed]. So I keep several, and I can nearly always lay my hands on one.

Then the pump. Switch it off before you start. With the pump running, the water is moving and the air moves with it. Let it settle. [wait needed]. Then the air collects where it should, at the top of each radiator.

Then find it. I go round and feel each radiator with a flat hand. Bottom first, then top. A radiator with air in it is warm at the bottom and cold along the top. Not cold all over. Just a band across the top. That band is the trapped air. The bigger the band, the more air there is.

Then start at the ground floor and work up. Air rises through the system. If you do the top floor first, the air from below just comes up and fills it again. So ground floor first. Then the first floor. Then the top.

At each radiator, the key goes on the valve at the top corner. Turn it slowly. The air hisses out. Then water. Close it. Then the next one.

When all of them are done, I switch the pump back on. I check the pressure. [pressure needed]. Then I go round once more with a flat hand.

That is it. It takes [time needed]. The noise stops. The heat reaches the top of every radiator again.

Tonight the house is quiet. The keys are back in their drawer. Most of them.
