I do not at all agree with those who say that looking for rooms is a hardship.  I think it one of the pleasantest things there is.  There is no better way that I know of to see human nature.  You go in at one door after another, and behind each door there is somebody, and each somebody is different from the last.  You see how people live.  You see what they think important, and what they do not.  You come away, I think, a broader person than you went in; more tolerant, more sympathetic, more inclined to believe that there is a good deal to be said for everybody.

Even the advertisements are entertaining.  I have read a great many of them.  Every room in them is "large."  Every room is "light" and "airy."  Every house is "select," and every neighbourhood is "refined."  Every landlady keeps "a private family."  Every one of them offers "all home comforts."  The remarkable thing is that there appear to be, in the whole of the city, no rooms that are small, or dark, or stuffy, and no house that is not select.  It is a very comforting picture.  I have never been able quite to reconcile it with the rooms.  But that, I suppose, is the fault not of the rooms but of me.

The most charming rooms of all to look at, though, are the old chambers of the lawyers in London.  I speak of the Inns.  You go in out of the noise of the street through an archway, and at once it is quiet.  There are old trees, and old grass, and old stone, and old brick gone soft in colour with the smoke of many years.  There are staircases worn hollow in the middle.  There are names painted up beside the doors, a whole column of them, most of them, I dare say, of men long gone.  The rooms themselves are panelled, and rather dark, and have deep windows, and very likely a fireplace that smokes.  They are not convenient.  Nobody has ever said that they are convenient.  But there is about them a kind of mellowness, of settled and dignified age, that I have met with nowhere else.  One would not need, I think, to take them.  It is enough to have looked at them.
