The first time I saw him he was standing near the front of the shop, talking.  I did not know at first who he was.  He was a short man, and stout, and quite bald; the light from the window fell upon the top of his head.  He was not, to look at, in the least what one would have expected.  He talked steadily, not loud, and as he talked he moved his hands.  He moved them all the time, evenly, one after the other, as though he were shaping what he said in the air before him and setting it down, piece by piece, where it belonged.  I remember the hands better than I remember anything he said, for I was not near enough to hear much of that.

Somebody told me who he was.  I looked again.  I confess it did not seem possible.

He was with a lady who was also, in her way, a writer: a society author, very much in the fashion at that time, whose books we sold a good many of.  Her husband was with them.  They stood about among the tables for a while, the three of them, and he went on talking, and the lady listened, and the husband listened too, or looked as if he did.  Then they went out together.  They went out arm in arm, the novelist in the middle, a short stout figure between the two of them, still talking, I think, as they passed through the door.  I watched them go up the street.

He came back afterwards, more than once, and alone.  And on these occasions he was another man altogether.  He did not talk.  He did not, so far as I could see, notice anybody.  He went to the shelves and he stayed there.  He took down a book and looked at it, at the title page, at the binding, at the print, turning it over; and put it back; and took down the next one.  He went along the shelves in this way, slowly, from one end to the other, entirely absorbed.  You could have spoken to him and I believe he would not have heard.  Once or twice I did go near him, to ask whether there was anything that I could find for him, and then thought better of it, and went away again.  It seemed to me that he was, just then, very busy; and that whatever it was that he was busy about, it was his own affair.
