The vertical threads are in. Now the cross threads. This is the weaving part, and it is where most darns go wrong.

Start at one corner of the patch. Take the needle over the first vertical thread, under the next, over the next. All the way across. Take a small stitch into the good wool at the edge. Turn. Come back the other way, under where you went over, over where you went under. Then again. Row after row, until the patch is filled in.

Do not pull tight. That is the one rule. It is tempting. Each row looks neater if you give it a tug. But the tension adds up. Pull every row a little and by the end the patch has drawn in on itself. It bunches. It puckers the sock around it. Then it rubs when you walk, which is how you got the hole in the first place. Leave each row lying flat and a little loose. Push it up against the last one with the tip of the needle. Let the weave hold it, not the pull.

When it is done, it looks like a small square of cloth set into the sock. It is. You made it.

I find the time goes quietly. A sock, a bulb, a needle, an evening. No screen. Just the next row, and the next. It is restful in a way not much else is.

And it works. A heel I have darned lasts longer than the heel the sock came with. I have socks where the darn is the strongest part. The wool around it wears out first.
