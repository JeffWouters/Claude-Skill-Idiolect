The old seal came out in pieces. It had gone flat and hard, and it was cracked across one side. That was the drip. Water had been getting past that crack for a long time.

I took the pieces to the local hardware store. Take the old one with you. Seals look alike, and they are not. I held mine against the new ones until I found the match. It cost very little. [price needed] I bought two.

Then the reassembly. This is where you slow down. Everything goes back in the order it came out. I had laid the parts on a towel in a line, left to right, so the order was right there in front of me. New seal first. Then the valve. Then the handle. Hand tight to start. Then a gentle turn with the spanner. No more than that. Brass is soft. If you lean on it, you strip the thread. Then you have a bigger job than the one you started with.

Now the water. Do not open the supply valve all the way in one go. Open it a little. Listen. Let the pipe fill. The air comes out first, in coughs and spits. Then water. Then a bit more. Only then did I open it the rest of the way. I stood there and watched the spout.

Nothing. No drip. I watched it a while longer to be sure. Still nothing.

The repair itself was quick. The preparation was not. Clearing out the cupboard under the sink. Finding the right tools. Shutting off the water. Laying out the towel. Working out how the thing came apart. That took far longer than putting a new seal in. It is always the way with jobs like this. The doing is short. The getting ready is long.

The spare seal went into a small bag, and the bag went into the drawer with the spanner. The old pieces went in too, so I know what to ask for next time. Because there will be a next time. There always is.
