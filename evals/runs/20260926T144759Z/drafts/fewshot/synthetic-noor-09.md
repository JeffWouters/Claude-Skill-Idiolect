Every autumn the radiators start to knock. It happens the first week the heating comes on. Gurgles, ticks, a sound like water running in the wall. That is air. So I bleed them.

First, find a key. This is harder than it sounds. A radiator key is small and it goes missing. I have [number needed] of them now. One in the kitchen drawer, one on a hook, one in the toolbox. [locations needed] Even then, some years I can only find one.

Then feel each radiator. Put the heating on and wait for it to warm up. Then go round with a flat hand. Lay it on the front, low down, and move it up. Warm. Warm. Warm. Then cold. That cold band at the top is the air. It sits there and the hot water cannot get in. Some radiators are cold across the whole top. Some only in one corner. Some are fine. I do the whole house, every one, so I know which ones need doing.

Then switch the pump off. Turn the heating off at the boiler and let it settle. If the pump is running, it pushes air around the system while you are trying to let it out, and some of it goes straight back in. Or it pulls air in through the valve you have just opened. Give it a while. [time needed]

Then start at the bottom. Ground floor first. Then upstairs. Air rises, so it collects at the top of the house. If you do the top first and then the bottom, the air you let out downstairs just pushes up to fill the ones you have already done. Bottom to top, and you are chasing it the right way.

At each radiator, a cloth under the valve. Key in. A slow quarter turn. The hiss. Then water. Then shut it.

It takes most of an afternoon. The knocking stops. Until next autumn.
