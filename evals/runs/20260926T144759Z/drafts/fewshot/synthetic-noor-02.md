I used to work on the train. Laptop open, emails, the first few jobs of the day done before I got in. I gave that up. It was not a big decision. I just stopped. And what I got back was time that nobody can reach. No signal worth the name in stretches. No one expects a reply. I sit, and I look out of the window. That is all. It is the only part of the day that belongs to no one else.

That is when the repetition starts to pay. If you see the same thing every day, you see when it changes. The small things. The trees along the line go from bare to green to yellow and back again, and you watch every step of it. One morning the fields are white with frost and the ditches are skinned with ice. The next week they are not. A wall that was grey on Monday has fresh paint on it by Thursday. New letters, new colours.
