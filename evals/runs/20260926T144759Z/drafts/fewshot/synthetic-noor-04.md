Put a cloth under the valve first. An old towel is fine. Then fit the key to the square pin at the top corner of the radiator and turn it slowly. A quarter turn. No more. You will hear a hiss. That is the air. Let it go.

Keep the cloth ready. The hiss will stop and there will be a spit. Then water. A little dribble at first, then a steady bead. That is the moment. Close the valve. Gently. Do not force it. The pin is small and it is usually brass, and brass is soft. Crank it shut and you can shear it, or crush the seat, and then it will weep for ever. Snug is enough. Wipe it dry, and check it again after a minute. If it is still dry, move on.

Do every radiator that needs it. Then go and look at the boiler.

Letting air out lets pressure out too. The gauge on the boiler will have dropped. Mine always does. So now you top it up. That is what the filling loop is for. It is the flexible hose under the boiler with a valve at each end. [description of loop depends on boiler model] Open one valve. Then open the other, slowly, and watch the needle on the gauge. It climbs. Stop at about 1.2 bar. Close both valves. Check the needle again. It should sit still.

Then turn the heating back on and wait.

The difference is easy to feel. Before, the radiators were hot at the bottom and cold at the top. After, the heat runs right across them. Top to bottom, end to end. The rooms warm up evenly. No more cold corner by the window.

It does not last. The air comes back. It comes back every year. I do not know exactly where it comes from, and it does not matter. So I do this every year. Same key, same cloth, same gauge. It is not a big job. It is just one that has to be done again.
