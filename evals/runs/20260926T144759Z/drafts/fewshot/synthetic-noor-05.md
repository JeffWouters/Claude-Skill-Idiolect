Wool socks go at the heel first. Then the toe. Mine do, anyway. A small hole at first. Then a bigger one. You can throw them out. Or you can darn them.

You need three things. A long needle, with an eye big enough for yarn. Yarn that matches the sock. If it is too thick, you will feel the lump every time you walk. If it is too thin, it will not hold. And something round to stretch the sock over. I use an old light bulb. It is the right shape, it is smooth, and it was in the drawer anyway.

Push the bulb into the sock and bring the hole up over the top of it. The wool should be stretched, but not tight. Now look at the hole. Then look past it.

This is the part people miss. The hole is not the problem. The hole is the end of the problem. Around it, the wool has gone thin. That is the next hole. If you only fill the gap, the darn pulls on the thin wool around it, and the thin wool gives. Then you have a bigger hole with a darn in the middle of it.

So you mend the whole weak patch. Not only the gap.

First mark out the area. I run a line of small running stitches round it, well out into the good wool. [how far out needed] That is the boundary.

Then the vertical threads. Start at one side of the boundary. Take a small stitch in the good wool at the top. Carry the yarn straight down, across the hole, and take a small stitch in the good wool at the bottom. Turn. Back up again, right next to the first thread. Down again. Up again. Keep the threads parallel. Keep them close. Side to side, until the whole patch is covered.

That is half the darn. The other half goes across.
