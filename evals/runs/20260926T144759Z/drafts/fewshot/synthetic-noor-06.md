The tap had been dripping for weeks. An old brass one, the kitchen tap. I had got used to the sound. Then one night I put a jug under the spout before bed. In the morning it had [amount needed] in it. That is not a drip. That is water down the drain every hour of every day. So that was that.

First, the drain. Put the plug in. Then lay a cloth over the plug. Small parts from a tap are exactly the size of a plughole. If one falls, you want it to land on cloth, not go down the pipe.

Then the water. The shut-off valve for the tap was under the sink, behind the bottles and the bucket. It had not been turned in years. It was stiff. I did not force it with a wrench. I worked it back and forth by hand, a little each way, until it gave. Then I turned it off all the way. Then I opened the tap to check. A cough of air, a last trickle, then nothing. Good.

Now the handle. There was a cap on top. I prised it off with a small flat screwdriver. Under it, a screw. Out came the screw and the handle lifted off. Everything went on a tea towel, in a row, in the order it came off.

Then the valve assembly. This is where you have to be careful. The nut is tight, and when you turn it, the whole tap wants to turn with it. Brass is soft, and an old tap is not as solid on the sink as it looks. If it twists, you can crack the seal underneath or strain the pipes. So brace it. One hand holds the body of the tap still. The other turns the spanner. Short, steady pulls. No jerking.

It came loose on the [number needed] try. Then it turned by hand. Then it was out, and I could see the seal at the bottom.
