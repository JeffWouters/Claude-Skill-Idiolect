Every autumn, when the heating comes back on after the summer, our radiators announce that they need attention. They tick, gurgle and knock, and some of them heat only halfway. That is the sign that air has collected inside them, and bleeding them has become a fixed part of the season.

The first step is finding a radiator key, which is harder than it should be. They are small, they look like nothing much, and they go missing. So I keep several spares, in different places around the house [example needed: where they are kept]. Having more than one also means I am never stuck halfway through the job because the only key has vanished.

Before opening anything, I switch off the pump, or turn the heating off, and let things settle for a while. With the pump running, water is being pushed around the system, and it is harder to get the air out cleanly. With the pump off, the air rises to the top of each radiator and stays there, ready to be released.

Then I go round and feel each radiator. You do not need any instruments for this. Run your hand from the bottom to the top. A radiator with trapped air is warm below and noticeably cold in its upper zone, and the border between the two tells you roughly how much air there is. The ones that are warm all over can be left alone. The cold-topped ones get bled.

I work upward from the ground floor. Air moves up through the system, so starting at the bottom and finishing at the top means I am not pushing air into radiators I have already done. At each one, I open the bleed valve slightly, let the air hiss out, and close it again as soon as water appears.

When the house is done, I check the boiler pressure and switch everything back on. The noises fade, the radiators heat evenly, and the heating is ready for winter until the same time next year.
