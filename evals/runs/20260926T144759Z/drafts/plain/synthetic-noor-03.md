There is a sheet of paper on our refrigerator that has been running the household for years. Not the same sheet, of course, but the same idea, where nobody can miss it.

It is divided into three parts. The first is groceries: the things we have run out of or are about to. The second is quick chores, the small jobs that take hardly any time but somehow never happen unless they are written down. The third is larger projects, the ones that need a weekend or a trip to the hardware store or simply some courage to start. Having them on one page means you can see, at a glance, what the house needs from you today and what it needs from you eventually.

The one rule that makes it work is simple: whoever uses up an item writes it down. Finish the milk, write milk. Use the last of the coffee, write coffee. It sounds obvious, but it removes the entire argument about who should have noticed. The person who knows it is gone is the person who records it, and the list stays accurate without anyone having to police it.

We did not arrive at paper straight away. We tried phone apps, several of them, and they all had the same problem: the list lived inside a phone, and a phone is somewhere you have to go looking. We tried a notebook, which ended up in a drawer. We tried a whiteboard, which seemed perfect until things got wiped by accident and nobody could remember what had been on it. Paper on the fridge wins because it is visible. You walk past it all day long. You open the fridge, you see the list, and the gap between noticing and recording shrinks to nothing. It needs no charging, no syncing and no login. When it is full, we start a new one. It is not clever, and that is exactly why it has lasted.
