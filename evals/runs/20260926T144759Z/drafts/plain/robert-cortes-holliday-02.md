Anyone who has gone looking for rented rooms in the city will have met a great many unhappy people doing the same thing, and I have come to think that most of their unhappiness is of their own making. They are not really looking for rooms. They are looking for grand quarters. They carry in their heads a picture of lofty ceilings, polished floors, a view, a quiet street and a sympathetic landlord, all at a rent they can comfortably afford, and every door they knock on is measured against that picture and found wanting. Naturally they come away disappointed. The city does not keep a supply of palaces for people of modest means, and it never has.

The luxury flats are another matter, and a puzzling one. They go up in block after block, and they are let, apparently without difficulty, at rents that seem to me steep beyond reason. Who pays them? That is the mystery I have never solved. One does not know these people. They are not famous; their names are never in the papers; they do not, so far as anyone can see, do anything remarkable. Yet there are evidently a great many of them, enough to fill building after building, and every one of them manages each month to find a sum that would keep a more ordinary tenant for a good deal longer. I have sometimes stood outside such a building in the evening and looked at the lighted windows and wondered what all those unknown people do for a living, and how they do it so well.

The rest of us go elsewhere. Not long ago I followed an advertisement to a house off a lane in Greenwich Village. It was shabby in the way that only an old house can be shabby, with the paint gone thin and the stairs worn into hollows, and the room I had come to see was at the top. The door was opened for me and I went in. The last tenant had left, but he had not left entirely. His habits were written all over the place. I stood there for a while reading the evidence, as one might read a letter left open on a table, and then I thanked the person who had shown me up and went back down the worn stairs.

I did not take the room. But I did not regret the visit, either. A room that tells you so plainly who lived in it before is, in its way, better company than a luxury flat that tells you nothing at all.
