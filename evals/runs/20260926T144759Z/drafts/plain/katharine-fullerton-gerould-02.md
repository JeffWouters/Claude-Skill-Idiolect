Science no longer asks to be treated as one way of knowing among several. It presents itself as the way of knowing, a universal method that any subject may adopt and every subject should. The result has been a quiet displacement of culture, carried out less by argument than by imitation.

Look at what has happened to the humanities. The study of literature, history and languages has been pressed into the shape of a laboratory discipline. What can be counted is counted; what can be classified is classified; and what resists both is set aside as unscholarly. A poem becomes an occasion for tracing sources, tabulating forms and establishing dates. These are respectable tasks, but they are the scaffolding of the subject, not the subject. A student can master all of them and never learn why the poem was worth reading. The technical work has grown until it fills the room, and the thing it was meant to serve has been shown out.

The trouble is that the most important questions in art are not questions of measurement. Whether a picture is beautiful, whether a sentence is well made, whether a piece of music moves us for good reasons or cheap ones: none of this can be settled with instruments. Physical measurement can tell us the dimensions of a canvas and the chemistry of its pigments. It cannot tell us what the painting is worth. Judgment of that kind depends on trained perception, long acquaintance and comparison, and a willingness to commit oneself to an opinion that cannot be proved. To call such judgment unscientific is accurate. To call it therefore worthless is a confusion.

I want to be plain about what I am objecting to. I have no quarrel with science in its own field. Its methods have given us knowledge of the physical world that no earlier age possessed, and it would be foolish to wish that undone. My complaint is against a claim that science makes, or that is made on its behalf: that its method is the only respectable form of thought, and that every other kind of inquiry must either become scientific or admit itself to be mere feeling. That claim is not itself a scientific finding. It is an ambition for total intellectual dominance, and culture is one of the things it has cost us.
