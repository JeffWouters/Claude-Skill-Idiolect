Every effort to end war runs into the same obstacle, and the obstacle is youth. Not any particular young person, and not the young out of wickedness, but the plain fact that each generation arrives full of an enthusiasm the previous one has spent. That enthusiasm keeps undoing the careful work of peace.

Look at who attends a peace conference. The halls fill with older listeners, and the older listeners are persuaded. They hear the arguments about cost and suffering and waste, and they agree, because the arguments match what they already know. The speeches do their work on exactly the people who needed them least. Young men, meanwhile, stay away. They are not in the audience to be convinced, and nothing said there reaches them. When the next occasion for war arrives, it is their opinion, not the conference's, that carries the day.

One could almost propose, half seriously, a remedy that goes to the root. If the warlike impulse belongs to youth and fades in middle age, then perhaps the answer is to extend middle age. Let people reach it sooner and stay in it longer, and the appetite for fighting might simply have no season in which to grow. It is a fanciful idea, of course. But it names the difficulty more honestly than many serious proposals do. The trouble is not a shortage of good arguments. It is a shortage of listeners in the frame of mind to accept them.

The pattern is old. There is a biblical story of a king who, faced with a hard decision, took counsel from the elders who had served before him, and then ignored them. He turned instead to the young men he had grown up with, his peers, and followed their advice. Their advice was bold and harsh, and the result was division: the kingdom split, and it did not come back together. [example needed: the king's name and passage, if the piece is to cite them]

That story could stand at the head of every account of a failed peace. The elders spoke sensibly. The young spoke with more heat and more confidence, and heat and confidence are what move a crowd. Until the peacemakers find a way to address the young men who stay away, their best work will keep being undone by the next generation to come of age.
