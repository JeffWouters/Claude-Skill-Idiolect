The patch goes on from the middle out. I press it with both thumbs and count to sixty. Then I roll the handle of the screwdriver over it, hard. The foil comes off. The thin plastic stays.

Here is the count since I started keeping it. 11 punctures in six years. Nine on the back. Seven thorns, two bits of glass, one pinched tube that was my own fault, one I never found. Six patches used from this tin. A new tube costs about 7 euros at the shop by the station.

But I do not buy the tube.

The shop would change it in ten minutes. Jan next door says so every time. He leans on the fence and says, just bring it in and have done with it. I think he is right about the time. The patch takes most of an hour. But the tube is fine apart from one small hole. And I like the step, and the bowl, and the bubbles coming up in a line. It is a job with an end to it. Not many are.

Putting it back is the same job in reverse. A little air in the tube first, so it keeps its shape. Valve in. Then the tube into the tyre, and the tyre onto the rim with my thumbs. The last part is hard. I do not use a lever for it. A lever pinches the tube. That was the pinched one in the count.

Then the pump. 4 bar for the back. I check it with my thumb and then with the gauge. The thumb is usually right. Then the wheel goes back in. Chain on, nuts tight, brake closed. I spin the wheel. A soft tick from the valve each time round.

This evening the bicycle is back in the shed. The bowl is upside down on the step to dry. The tin is on the shelf by the door with five patches left. One cracked lever, still.
