So then the cleaning. Every joint vacuumed, then wiped with a damp sponge. Grout will not stick to dust. It sits on top of it and lifts in a month.

The mixing is the part people rush. Water first, then powder. Stir until it is like thick yoghurt. Not runny. Not stiff. Then leave it five minutes and stir again. That is the rule: let it slake. Slaking is the time the powder takes to drink the water. I mix small amounts, about a cup at a time. More than that goes hard in the bucket before I reach it.

The float goes on at an angle. You push the grout across the joints, not along them. Diagonal one way, then the other. It fills the gaps and leaves a grey film on the face of the tiles. The film does not matter yet.

Twenty minutes later I come back with the sponge. Wrung out almost dry. One pass, then turn the sponge. One pass, then rinse. A wet sponge drags the grout out of the joints. I learned that the first time too. The joints went hollow and pale.

Then the handle of a teaspoon. I run it along each line to shape it. A shallow curve, just below the tile edge. Water runs off a curve. It sits on a flat line.

Here is the count. 41 tiles. About 11 metres of joint. Four cups of grout. Six rinses of the bucket. One tile reset. One broken fingernail.

The last step is the haze. An hour after the sponge, a dry cloth. The film comes off like chalk from a board. The tiles looked whiter than they have in years. They are not. The new grout makes them look so.

There is a cheaper way. You can buy a pen that paints old grout white. My neighbour did his last year. It looked fine for a month. Then he stood at the fence and said, it wants doing again already.
