This is the part I watch. The boil rises up the pan and stays there. It spits. It smells of burnt sugar and orange oil. I stand back a little. I keep three saucers in the freezer.

Here is the test. After 10 minutes I drop a spoonful on a cold saucer. I wait one minute. I push it with a fingertip. If it wrinkles, it is ready. If it runs, I boil it for five more and try the next saucer. Most years it is the second saucer. Once it was the fourth.

The jars wait in a low oven. I let the marmalade stand in the pan for ten minutes first. That way the peel does not float to the top. I ladle it through a jam funnel and fill each jar to a centimetre from the rim. Then the lids go on hot.

Here is the count. 2 kilos of oranges. 4 kilos of sugar. 19 jars, most years. 16 the year I let it catch on the bottom.

But it would be cheaper to buy it. I know that. A good jar in the supermarket is 3.50 euros and it tastes fine. I bought one once, in a March when mine had run out. It was sweet and even. The peel was all the same size.

I keep making it because of the one week. The oranges come and I have to be ready. The pan comes down from the top shelf. The board comes out. The kitchen smells of it for days. It is the one job in the year that will not wait for me.

I give some away. Four jars go over the fence, and a jar of honey comes back. One goes to a daughter. The rest go on the larder shelf in two rows.

Each lid gets a label in capitals: MARMALADE, JAN, and the year. I have never needed to write more than that.
