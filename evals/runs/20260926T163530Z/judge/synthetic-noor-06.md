# Judge packet synthetic-noor-06

## The passage (by the author)

Ilse lives next door. Her path is the same length as mine and runs beside it, with a low fence between. She is past eighty. She still goes out for bread every morning at eight. So I do her path as well. I have done it for six winters now.

I do hers the same way. Step to gate, middle line first, then the sides. Her step is steeper than mine. It has a crack along the front edge where the frost got in. I clear half a metre in front of it and grit it twice. That is the rule: the step before the path.

She has never asked. The first winter she came to the window and lifted a hand. The next morning there was a jar of plum jam on my step. The lid was tied in cloth. The jam comes every winter now. One jar for each fall of snow.

But I would clear it without the jam. The council runs a snow service for older people. It costs nothing and it comes by about ten, most days. I told her about it once. She said, the council comes too late for the bread. So it is me, at seven.

I think I keep doing both paths for the order of it. A street under snow has no marks at all. By eight there are two clean lines to two front doors. And one set of small boots going out to the corner and back.

The shovel goes back in the shed wet. I stand it blade down on a sack so the edge does not rust. The broom goes beside it. Gloves on the radiator, boots on newspaper by the door.

It snowed again last night, not much. Three centimetres or so. This morning I swept both paths with the broom and left the shovel where it was. On my step, a jar of plum jam. The lid tied in blue cloth.
