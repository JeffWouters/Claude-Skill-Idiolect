Anyone who has worked on a software team has seen it happen. A new service, module or feature needs a name, and the discussion runs longer than the one about what the thing should actually do. People who agreed quickly on the design argue at length about a single word. From the outside it looks like bikeshedding, and sometimes it is. But accuracy alone does not explain how intense these debates get. If the only goal were a correct label, most names would be settled quickly, because most candidates are roughly correct.

What the debate is really about is commitment. A name is a statement of what something is, and by the same stroke it is a statement of what it is not. Call a component a "scheduler" and you have implied it schedules things, and also that it does not do the other jobs someone might later want to add to it. Every name closes off some future directions. People sense this, even when they cannot put it into words, and the ones who care about those directions push back.

The usual escape is to pick a provisional name and move on. That feels sensible: nobody wants to hold up the work over a word, and you can always rename it later. In practice, "later" rarely comes. The provisional name ends up in code, in dashboards, in documentation and in the way people talk in meetings. Before long it has hardened, and renaming it now means touching all of those places. So the placeholder stays, and it starts to shape the scope of the thing it was only meant to label.

The clearest example is the word "platform". Calling something a platform tells everyone else in the organisation that it is meant to be built upon. That is an invitation, and people accept it. Requests start arriving from teams you never planned to serve, each reasonable on its own terms, each pulling the work in a new direction. The name did that, not the design.

So the long argument over a name is often a scope argument wearing a disguise. Treating it as one, and saying out loud what the name commits you to, tends to make it shorter and more useful.
