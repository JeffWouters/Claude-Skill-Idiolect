The New Forest has had a long life and only one adventure. Or rather, it has had only one that anybody remembers, which for a forest comes to the same thing. Centuries ago King William Rufus went hunting there and did not come home. Somebody killed him, and nobody has ever found out who. The trees have been living on the story ever since. It is not much to live on for so many generations, but they make it go a long way, as old families do with a single distinguished ancestor.

We had no intention of solving the mystery. It has been in abler hands than ours, and has been all the better for being left alone. A murder that is cleared up becomes a matter for the police; a murder that is never cleared up becomes a matter for the imagination, and is at the service of every passer-by.

There was a road through the forest, and it was full of motor cars. They were going somewhere in a great hurry, and I have no doubt they arrived. We let them go. It seemed to us that the best way to see a forest was to get lost in it, and we turned aside among the old trees, which had not been planted for anybody's convenience and did not propose to be hurried. We were grateful that the land belonged to the Crown. A private owner would long ago have improved it. The Crown, being above such ambitions, had been content to let it stay wild, and there are times when a little royal neglect is the best thing that can happen to a landscape.

We ate our luncheon under a great tree, and felt ourselves to be poachers of a very mild kind. Not far off some women were gathering firewood. They did it without apology, for it was their right, and had been their right for longer than anyone could tell. It was the only law in the forest that seemed older than the killing of the King.
