First find the hole. Once it is found, the patch is the easy part. I rough up the rubber around the hole with the little square of sandpaper from the kit. Not too hard. Then a thin smear of glue, wider than the patch. And then I wait. This is where people go wrong. The glue has to go dull before the patch goes on. If it is still shiny, the patch will slide and lift. When it is ready I press the patch down from the middle outwards. I hold it for a good while. Then I peel off the backing.

Next the tyre. I run my fingers round the inside of it first. Slowly. Whatever made the hole may still be in there. I put a little air in the tube so it holds its shape. I tuck it in, valve first. Then I work the bead back over the rim with my thumbs. The last bit is always the tightest. A lever can pinch the tube here, so I go carefully. Then I pump it up, a bit at a time, and check the tyre sits evenly all the way round. Then up to full pressure.
