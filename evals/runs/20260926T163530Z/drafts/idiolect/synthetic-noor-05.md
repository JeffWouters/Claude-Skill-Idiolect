The compost heap has two bays. One fills while the other rots. That is the whole system. It came to me from [example needed: who built it first]. They built it the same way and turned it the same way. I have changed nothing. Not even the wood.

All sorts goes in over the year. Grass cuttings in summer. Leaves in autumn. Peelings and tea leaves from the kitchen, all year round. Cut stems, chopped small. Spent pots of annuals. [example needed: what else goes in]. It all goes on top. The pile sinks as fast as I add to it.

Here is the hard part. Picking the day. It must be dry. Wet compost is heavy, and it sticks to the fork. It must not be too hot either. I want a still, cool day after a dry spell. I wait for one. However long it takes.

Then I open up the full bay. I take the front boards out one at a time. And there it is. The whole year in layers. Black and fine at the bottom. Brown and half gone in the middle. The top is still plain to see. Stalks. Eggshells. A tea bag or two.

The rule is simple. The least rotted stuff goes to the bottom of the empty bay. The top of the old heap is the base of the new one. It gets the most time to rot. The good stuff below it stays where it is. Or it goes on the beds.
