I have been invited to go over a large funeral home.  When I mentioned this to my friends they considered it the most entertaining piece of news they had heard in a month, and they have not yet finished being entertained by it.  [example needed: what the friends said]  It is a curious thing.  We are, most of us, perfectly willing to joke about death; there are more jokes about it, I suppose, than about almost anything else in human experience.  Yet let a man so much as mention an undertaker and the company shifts uneasily in its chairs.  We laugh at the thing itself, and we are afraid of the man who merely attends to it.  Why this should be so I cannot pretend to say.

My host was a quiet, courteous person, not in the least funereal, who took my curiosity--and, I dare say, my nervousness--in very good part.  He told me of a belief which, it seems, is extremely widespread: that an establishment such as his is certain to lower the value of property throughout the neighbourhood.  People are quite convinced of it.  But the belief, he explained, is very often simply mistaken.  [example needed: the host's evidence on property values]  He spoke without resentment, as a man speaks of a misunderstanding he has encountered a great many times before and expects to encounter a great many times again.

There was one thing, however, that did seem to try his patience.  That was the pun.  Visitors, he said, will make jokes about his profession.  They cannot help it.  They make the same jokes, over and over, and each of them believes he is the first person in history to have thought of it.  I gathered that the funeral director has heard every one of these, so to speak, until he is heartily tired of the whole collection.  I resolved, then and there, to keep my own contribution to myself.
