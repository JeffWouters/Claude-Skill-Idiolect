# Judge packet synthetic-noor-02

## The passage (by the author)

The bicycle gets a puncture about twice a year. Almost always the back tyre. I mend it at the kitchen door, on the step, with the door open behind me. The light is good there until four.

The kit lives in a tin that once held throat sweets. It was my father's. Inside are six patches, a tube of glue, a stub of chalk, a square of sandpaper and three plastic levers. One lever is cracked. I have meant to replace it for two winters.

This time it was a thorn. I felt it on the way home on Tuesday. A soft thud through the saddle. Then the slow sag. I walked the last kilometre.

Here is the order.

First the wheel comes off. I turn the bicycle over and stand it on the saddle and the bars. I undo the brake. I loosen the nuts with the 15 millimetre spanner. Then the chain has to be lifted off the small cog. That is the fiddly part. Your fingers go black at once.

So then the tyre. I let out what air is left and push the valve up into the rim. Two levers go in, a hand's width apart. You hook them under the bead. That is the stiff edge that holds the tyre to the rim. Then you run the third lever round. The tyre comes away like a lid off a jar.

The tube comes out last. Valve first, or it tears.

Finding the hole is the slow part. I pump the tube up hard and hold it by my ear. Nothing. So I fill the washing-up bowl at the sink and carry it out to the step. I feed the tube through the water a hand's length at a time. Bubbles. A thin line of them, very fine. I mark the place with chalk. A big cross.

Now push the valve up through the rim and pull the tube out. Gently. Work round from the valve.

Then the bowl of water. Pump a bit of air into the tube. Not much. Enough to give it shape. Then push it under the water, a hand's width at a time, and watch. You are looking for bubbles. A thin line of them. Sometimes it is one bubble every few seconds. Be patient. When you find it, keep your thumb on the spot. Dry the tube. Then mark it with the crayon or chalk from the kit. A cross, big enough to find again. Do not skip that. You will lose it otherwise.
