I turn the heap in autumn. There are two bays. One is full, one is empty. The job is to move the full one into the empty one. That is all turning is.

A heap needs air. The things that break it down need air to work. Without it, the middle goes wet and heavy. It starts to smell. I know this because I left mine alone for a long spell. [example needed: how long the heap was left and what it was like when opened]. I will not do that again.

When a heap is working, you can tell. Push a fork into the middle and it comes out warm. On a cold morning, it steams. Inside, it is dark and crumbly. You can still see the odd eggshell and a few stalks. Worms everywhere. It smells of soil and not much else. The outside is always slower. That is why you turn it. The dry edges go into the middle and the middle goes to the edges.
