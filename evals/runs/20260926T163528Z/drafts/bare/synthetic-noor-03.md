First, the juice. I cut each orange in half and squeeze it. The juice goes into the pan. The pips and the pith go into a square of muslin. I scrape the halves out with a spoon to get all of it. Then I tie the muslin up into a bag. It looks like rubbish. It is not. That bag is what makes the marmalade set.

Then the peel. This, for me, is the slow part. I slice it as fine as I can with a sharp knife. Thin shreds, not chunks. Thick peel stays chewy however long you cook it. [time needed: how long the slicing takes]. The peel goes into the pan with the juice and the water, and the bag goes in with it. Then the lid goes on and it all sits overnight.

The next day I bring it up to a simmer. And then I leave it there. The peel has to go soft before any sugar goes in. Properly soft. It should squash flat between finger and thumb. Sugar stops it softening. Whatever state the peel is in when the sugar goes in, that is how it stays.

I learned that the hard way. [past failure needed: what happened the time it was rushed]. So now I wait, however long it takes.

When the peel is soft, I lift the bag out into a bowl. I let it cool a little. Then I squeeze it hard against the side with a wooden spoon. Thick, cloudy stuff runs out. That is the pectin. It all goes back into the pan.
