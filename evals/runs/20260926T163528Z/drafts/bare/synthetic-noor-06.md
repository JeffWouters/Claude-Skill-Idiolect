Start at the house end. That way you are never standing on snow you still have to clear. You also have a clear way back to the door if you need it. Then cut a line down the centre of the path. One shovel wide, all the way to the far end. Now you have two strips of snow, one each side. Work outward from the middle. Push the shovel along the cleared line and into the snow. Lift, turn, throw.

Throw downwind. If you throw into the wind, it comes straight back at you. In your face, down your collar. Look at which way the snow is blowing first. Then pick your side.

Take partial loads. A full shovel of wet snow is heavy. Much heavier than it looks. Half a shovel is plenty. It feels slower. It is not. You keep going for longer and your back is fine the next day. Bend the knees. Keep the load close.

When the snow is off, sweep. A stiff broom gets the last thin layer up. Leave it and it packs down under your feet. By evening it is ice. Then spread the grit. A thin scatter is enough. Get it into the edges. [example needed: where people slip on this path].

Then I stood back. The path was clear. The rest of the street was still white.
