It has become something of a fashion among the critics to announce that Mr. Chesterton has diminished.  He has repeated himself too frequently, they explain; the paradox has worn threadbare, the conjuring trick is understood.  Very well.  I went to hear him lecture, and I am obliged to report that he does not appear diminished.  He appears, if anything, considerably larger than the critics have ever been.  He occupies the platform somewhat as a thunderhead occupies a summer afternoon.  One suspected that the lectern had been provided principally so that he might have something to lean upon, and that the lectern was not altogether confident of the arrangement. His subject, as nearly as I could make it out, was a quarrel with fatalism.  There is a notion abroad, he said in effect, that humanity is merely one more item in nature, carried along like a leaf upon a stream, and that it had better make its peace with the current.  Mr. Chesterton will have none of it.  Man, he insisted, stands apart from nature; he is the solitary creature in it who regards the stream and demands to know where it is going.  The cow does not argue with the pasture.  I do not know that the audience was converted, but it was certainly entertained, and perhaps entertainment is the beginning of conversion.  He laughed a good deal at his own jokes, and so did everybody else.

Afterwards I made my way round to the back of the hall.  There the difficulty was not to discover Mr. Chesterton, whom it would be impossible to overlook, but to approach him.  Between him and the remainder of the world stood a considerable company of eager young women, each of whom, I gathered, had something of the first importance to communicate.  I don't know what it was; I never got near enough to hear.  Presently a host appeared, a quiet and capable gentleman, and took the celebrated visitor in charge, much as a tugboat takes charge of an ocean liner.  Gently, firmly, with a word here and a smile there, he navigated him through the assembly and into a dressing room.  The door closed upon them, and the young women looked at one another.
