The rear wheel is the awkward one. First I shift down to the smallest cog. That takes the tension off the chain. Then I open the brake and undo the axle. I lift the frame and the wheel drops out. The chain needs a small push off the cog. That leaves oil on my fingers.

Then the tyre. I let out whatever air is left. I push one lever under the bead, opposite the valve, and hook it on a spoke. I put the second lever in the width of a hand along. Then I run it round the rim. One side of the tyre comes off. The other side stays on. I push the valve up through the rim and pull the inner tube out.

Now the leak. You can pump the tube up and listen for a hiss. It is hard to hear. So I use a bowl of water. I pump the tube up hard and push it under, a short length at a time. I turn it slowly and watch. Nothing, nothing, nothing. And then a thin line of bubbles. That is the hole.

I dry the tube and mark the spot straight away. A cross in chalk or a biro, big enough to see. If I look away without marking it, I lose it. It is a small hole and the tube all looks the same.
