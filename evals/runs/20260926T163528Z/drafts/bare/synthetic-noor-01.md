I start with the bowl. I run it under the hot tap and dry it. A cold bowl slows everything down and the dough sulks in it. Then the water. I do not use a thermometer. I hold my wrist under the tap and wait until it feels like nothing much.

Not warm and not cool. If it feels hot on the wrist, it is too hot. Too hot kills the yeast. Too cold only makes you wait a bit longer for the rise.

Then the flour goes in the bowl. I put the salt on one side and the yeast on the other. They must not touch at first. So I keep them apart until the water goes in. Then I mix it all with one hand into a rough, shaggy lump.

Now the kneading. I tip the dough onto a clean worktop. I push it away with the heel of my hand, fold it back towards me and turn it a quarter. Push, fold, turn. It is dull work and it takes a while. [time needed: how long the kneading takes].

At first the dough is sticky and ugly. It clings to your fingers and the worktop. This is where most people go wrong. They reach for the flour bag and dust the worktop and the dough and their hands. It feels like help. It is not. Every handful goes into the dough and makes the loaf drier and heavier.

The stickiness goes away by itself if you keep working. The dough slowly tightens. It turns smooth and springy under your hand. And then it stops sticking. No extra flour needed.
