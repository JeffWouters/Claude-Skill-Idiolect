It is not easy to be a good citizen in these days. Time was when a man who obeyed the laws, paid his taxes and minded his own business could hold up his head among his neighbors and expect their respect without further question. Nobody demanded more of him. If he did not set the world right, neither did he add to its disorder, and that was considered a creditable showing. He went to bed with a comfortable conscience and slept until morning.

That easy standard has passed. The conscientious person to-day finds himself confronted, not by one new duty, but by a whole procession of them, each insisting upon immediate attention and each supported by arguments he is unable to answer. He has scarcely acknowledged his obligation in one direction before he is reminded of three or four others of which he had never heard. It is as if all his debts had fallen due on the same day; he contemplates the accumulation of notes on his desk and wonders whether he is solvent. Each claim, perhaps, is just. It is their simultaneous arrival that is so disconcerting.

The consequence is that the moral life has become an expensive establishment. We used to reckon that the necessities of existence were food, clothing and shelter, and that a man who had provided these for his household had discharged his principal responsibilities. But the cost of living ethically has risen faster than the cost of living at all. A person of moderate means can still keep himself fed and housed. It is when he attempts to keep himself virtuous, according to the enlarged requirements of his contemporaries, that he discovers his income to be inadequate.

Nor is it sufficient to do nothing wrong. The modern critic takes little interest in the things you have done; he is concerned with what you have left undone. Whatever evil exists in the community, he will point out, exists in part because you did not prevent it. You are blamed for your neglect. And since there is no end to the things a man can neglect, there is no end to the blame.

I do not say the critic is mistaken. I only observe that good citizenship, which once was within the reach of any honest person, has become a strenuous profession.
