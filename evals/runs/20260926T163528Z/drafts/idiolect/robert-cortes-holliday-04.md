There is a hotel on lower Fifth Avenue which is not, strictly speaking, in New York at all.  It is in France.  It shows, I believe, no disposition whatever to come home.  You go in from the Avenue, and the Avenue is gone.  The cooking is French--not French as the menus of our larger establishments are French, in the spelling only, but French in the sauce.  I am no authority upon sauces.  I merely know when I have met a good one, and I have met a number of them there.

The company is at least as interesting as the food.  A great many of the diners are artists, or look as though they would like to be taken for artists, which in this neighbourhood amounts to much the same thing.  A remarkable proportion of them wear beards.  I do not mean the trim, apologetic beard of the Wall Street man who has been abroad; I mean the real article, full and unashamed, the kind of beard behind which a man may think in comfort.  The waiters, too, are of the old school.  They move among the tables with an air of having known your father, and of not having thought much of him. Nobody, so far as I could see, was in the least displeased with the arrangement.

Outside, at the curb, there is a sight you will hardly find elsewhere in the city to-day.  The old horse carriages wait there--cabs of a pattern one had supposed extinct--and on the boxes sit drivers in costume, with the patience of men who have outlived their century and do not intend to hurry about leaving it.  I have never been sure whether they are waiting for anybody in particular.  Perhaps they are simply waiting, as it were, for the past to come out after dinner.

The place has found its way into fiction.  A novel describes it under another name [example needed: the novel and its name for the hotel], and describes it, I think, pretty faithfully.  The novelist has not overlooked even the barbershop in the basement.  That, it seems to me, is the true test of a writer's acquaintance with a hotel.  Anybody can describe the dining-room.  It takes a regular guest to know where one goes to be shaved.
