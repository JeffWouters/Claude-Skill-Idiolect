So I start at the house end. Not the gate. That way I am never standing on snow I still have to clear. First I cut a line down the middle of the path, one blade wide, all the way to the gate. Then I work outward from that line. Left side, then right side. Short strips, one at a time.

Throw the snow downwind. It sounds obvious. It is easy to forget when you are shivering and in a hurry. Throw it into the wind and half of it comes back at you.

And do not fill the shovel. Take half a load. A third, if the snow is wet. Full loads of wet snow are punishing, and the back pays for it the next day. Lift with the legs. Turn the feet, not the waist.

Then the sweeping. A stiff broom takes off the last thin layer the shovel leaves. This is the part that stops it freezing into a slippery sheet. Then grit. I spread it by hand, a thin scatter, edge to edge. [number needed: how much grit used].

This morning the path was a dark stripe between two white banks. The gate was clear. And the old steel shovel was still in the shed, dry.
