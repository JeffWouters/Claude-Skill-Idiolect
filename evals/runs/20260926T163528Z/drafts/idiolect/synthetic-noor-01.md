First I warm the bowl. I fill it with hot water from the tap and leave it a few minutes. Then I tip the water out and dry the bowl. A cold bowl slows the yeast. A warm one gets it started.

Then the water. I do not use a thermometer. I test it on the inside of my wrist. It should feel like almost nothing. Not hot. Not cold. Just slightly warm. Too hot and it kills the yeast. So I err on the cool side.

The flour goes into the bowl. The salt goes on one side and the yeast on the other. They must not touch at the start. Salt draws the life out of yeast. So I keep them separate until the water goes in. Then I mix everything with one hand into a rough, wet mass.

Now the kneading. I tip the dough onto a clean worktop. No flour on it. I push the dough away with the heel of my hand. Then I fold it back over itself and give it a quarter turn. Push, fold, turn. Over and over. It takes about [number needed: minutes of kneading].

At first the dough is sticky. It clings to my fingers and the worktop. The urge is to throw on more flour. Do not. The extra flour upsets the balance of the dough. And the loaf comes out dry and heavy. So I leave it sticky and keep working. Gradually it stops clinging. It turns smooth and springy under my hand.

Tonight the dough sits in the warm bowl under a tea towel.
