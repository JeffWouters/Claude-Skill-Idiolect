I make it in [example needed: whose pan it was]. Here is how it goes.

First I cut each orange in half and squeeze out the juice. The juice goes into the pan. Then I scrape out the pips and the loose pith with a spoon. They go into a square of muslin. I tie it up into a small bag. This bag matters. The pips and pith hold the pectin. The pectin is what makes the marmalade set.

Then the peel. I slice it as finely as I can, with a sharp knife. Thin strips. Not chunks. It is slow work and there is no shortcut. I put the peel into the pan with the juice and the water. The muslin bag goes in too. Then I leave it all to soak overnight. The peel softens a little. The bag starts to give up its pectin.

The next day I bring it up to a gentle simmer. And I leave it there, uncovered. The peel has to be completely soft before any sugar goes in. I test it by pressing a strip between my finger and thumb. It should squash with no effort at all.

When the peel is soft I lift out the muslin bag. I let it cool in a bowl. Then I squeeze it hard between two spoons. A thick, cloudy jelly comes out of it. That is the pectin. It goes back into the pan.
