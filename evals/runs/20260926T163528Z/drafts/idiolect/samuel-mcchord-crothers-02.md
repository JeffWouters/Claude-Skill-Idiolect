It is not that we have grown wicked. It is rather that we have grown conscientious, and that the conscientious person now finds himself in the position of a man whose debts have all fallen due on the same morning. He does not deny any of them. He acknowledges each obligation as it is presented, and he would honestly like to discharge it. But he has not the means to settle them all at once, and the creditors are not disposed to wait.

The duties themselves are not new in the sense of being invented yesterday. What is new is that we see them. A good many of them existed before, but they lay comfortably out of sight, and a man who could not perceive them could hardly be blamed for neglecting them. Now there is no such shelter, for every morning brings intelligence of some wrong that he might help to right, some cause that has a claim upon his time and his purse, some neighbor near or distant whose welfare has somehow become his particular responsibility.

The result is that ethical living has become an expensive business. We used to hear a great deal about the high cost of the necessities of life. These are dear enough, but they are within the reach of a prudent man. The moral necessities are dearer. A person may maintain a roof over his head on a modest income; he will find it considerably harder to maintain a clear conscience on one.

There was a time when the terms were easier. If a man obeyed the law, paid what he owed and minded his own business, he was accounted a good citizen, and nobody thought of asking more of him. He was treated with respect. That was the whole of the bargain.

It is not so to-day. The critics of our society do not inquire what a man has done; they inquire what he has left undone, and whatever he has neglected, through ignorance or indolence or mere preoccupation with his own affairs, is laid to his charge. Minding one's own business, which was once a civic virtue, is now very nearly the chief of the vices. The good citizen of the old dispensation has become the respectable offender of the new.
