There are questions which no one has any business to ask, and yet they are asked every day, across tea-tables and over garden walls, with the most innocent air in the world. What is one to do with them? The moralist, I know, has his answer ready: one must either tell the truth or hold one's tongue. It sounds very well. Unfortunately it does not work.

For silence, in these matters, is seldom silent. Let one decline to answer, however civilly, and the questioner has his answer before one has finished declining. The refusal is itself a confession--and generally a more damaging confession than the plain fact would have been. [example needed] One might better have spoken, for then at least one would have chosen the words.

Now I do not see that a person who has no rightful claim to a piece of private knowledge is owed the truth about it. Why should he be? Prying is a kind of theft. The inquisitive acquaintance is picking a lock, and it has never yet been held that one must hand the burglar the spoons because he asked for them politely. If I put him off with a fiction, I have deceived him, certainly; but I have deceived him out of something that was never his to have.

But here I must draw a line, and draw it firmly. There is all the difference in the world between the lie that protects oneself and the lie that protects another. If I lie merely to spare my own dignity--to seem a little finer, or a little wiser, or a little better placed than I am--I am doing a shabby thing, and no amount of casuistry will make it otherwise. Vanity is not a secret one has any right to guard with falsehood. It is quite another matter when the secret belongs to someone else: a trouble confided in trust, which the questioner would carry off to the next house before dinner. There, I think, the lie may be excused--not praised, perhaps, but excused. One is not then saving one's own face. One is simply keeping shut a door that somebody else has asked one to keep shut; and I cannot believe that the recording angel, whatever else he may set down against us, will set down that.
