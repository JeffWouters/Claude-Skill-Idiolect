I buy the flour from [shop needed]. I keep it in [container needed], with the lid on tight. Flour takes on smells. It also takes on damp. A tight lid stops both.

First I warm the bowl. I fill it with hot water from the tap and leave it a minute. Then I tip the water out and dry it. A cold bowl slows everything down. The yeast sulks. The dough sits there. A warm bowl gets it going.

Then the water. I do not use a thermometer. I use my wrist. I run the tap and hold the inside of my wrist under it. It should feel warm. Not hot. About the heat of a bath you would be happy to sit in. If it feels hot, it is too hot, and it will kill the yeast. If you are not sure, go cooler. Cool water only makes the dough slow. Hot water makes it dead.

Salt and yeast go in on opposite sides of the bowl. Salt on the left, yeast on the right. Salt pulls the water out of yeast and weakens it. So they do not touch until the flour and water are mixed in between them. Then it does not matter.

Now the kneading. Tip the dough out onto a clean worktop. Push it away from you with the heel of your hand. Fold it back over itself. Turn it a quarter. Push again. Fold. Turn. [time needed]. Longer than you want.

It will be sticky. It will stick to your hands and to the worktop. Do not add more flour. That is the mistake. Every handful makes the loaf drier and tighter. Keep going instead. After a few minutes the dough tightens up on its own. It gets smooth. It stops sticking. It just takes longer than you think.
