The grout round the bath had been going for a while. It had gone dark in the corners. Then it started to crack. Small bits came away when I wiped the tiles. There was [example needed] in the joints along the bottom row that no amount of scrubbing would shift. Then one morning a tile moved under my hand. Just a little. That was it. The job had to be done.

The kit is not much. A grout rake. A bucket and a sponge. A grout float. New grout, and a tub of tile adhesive for the loose one. A vacuum for the dust. And a towel.

The towel is the important bit. Lay it in the bottom of the bath before you start. Fold it double. The last time I did a job like this I did not bother. I dropped [example needed] into the bath. It left a mark. The mark is still there. So now the towel goes in first. Every time.

Then the raking out. This is the slow part. You drag the rake along each joint, back and forth, until the old grout comes away. Not all of it. You want to go down about [depth needed]. Enough for the new grout to get a grip. Too shallow and it just flakes off again. Too deep and you risk loosening more tiles. I kept stopping to check with the tip of the rake. Joint after joint. Top to bottom, left to right.
