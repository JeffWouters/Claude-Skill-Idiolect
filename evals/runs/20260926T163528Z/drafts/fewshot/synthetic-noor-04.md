The tools are few. A maul, a wedge, a sledgehammer, a chopping block. The maul head was my [relative needed]'s. I put a new handle on it. [handle details needed]. The head is heavy and blunt. That is what you want. A maul does not cut. It bursts the log apart. The block is a round of elm. Elm is good for this. The grain twists, so it does not split itself when you miss.

The block has to be level. I did not think much about that at first. Then [near accident needed]. The log sat on a slope. When the maul hit, it did not split. It jumped. It went [direction needed]. It could just as easily have been my shin. So now I check the block before I start. I lay a log on it and look. If it rocks or leans, I dig the block in until it sits flat. Every time.

Then read the log. Look at the end grain. Dry logs have cracks running in from the bark. Those are your way in. The log is already starting to split there. Aim along the biggest crack. Not across it. If there is no crack, aim through the middle.

The swing is simple. Stand square, feet apart. The log a little in front of you. Lift the maul straight up over your head. Then let it drop. Do not force it. Let the weight do the work. Slide your top hand down the handle as it comes. Keep your eyes on the spot you want to hit. Hit it.

Some logs will not go. Knotty ones mostly. The maul bounces off. Or it sticks and you have to work it out again. That is when the wedge comes out. Tap it into a crack with the sledgehammer. Gently at first, until it holds. Then harder. You hear it before you see it. A creak. Then a crack. Then it falls in two.
