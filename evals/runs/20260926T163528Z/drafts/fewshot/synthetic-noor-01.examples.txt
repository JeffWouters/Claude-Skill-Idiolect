I am good at rain. I call it right about four times in five. I am bad at wind. I always think it will die down. It does not. And I am terrible at frost. The clear, still nights fool me every time. I go to bed thinking it is mild. I wake up to a white lawn and black dahlias.

Hold the blade at a low angle to the stone. About 15 degrees. That is lower than you think. If you lay a coin under the spine, that is close. Then push the blade away from you along the stone, edge first, as if you were slicing a thin layer off the top. Ten strokes. Then turn it over. Ten on the other side.

I cut the split ends off first. 15 centimetres from each end. Then I planed the tops. That took most of a weekend. Old scaffold boards are full of grit and paint and the odd nail. I found two nails with a magnet before I started. I missed a third. It took a chip out of the plane iron. So now I sweep a magnet over any old wood. Every time.
