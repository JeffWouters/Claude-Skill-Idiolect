Pushing a bike with a flat rear tyre is slow and awkward. The rim bumps on every kerb. I kept it off the rim as much as I could.

At home I turned the bike upside down. Saddle and bars on the floor, wheels in the air. Then I shifted onto the smallest sprocket at the back. That puts the chain where it is easiest to lift off. Undo the wheel. Then lift the chain off the sprocket and pull the wheel out and down. It is fiddly the first time.

Let what air is left out of the tube. All of it. Then the levers. Push the first one under the bead of the tyre, opposite the valve, and hook it on a spoke. Put the second one in a little way along. Lever that one and slide it round the rim. The bead comes over. Do one side only. Leave the other side on.

Now push the valve up through the rim and pull the tube out. Gently. Work round from the valve.

Then the bowl of water. Pump a bit of air into the tube. Not much. Enough to give it shape. Then push it under the water, a hand's width at a time, and watch. You are looking for bubbles. A thin line of them. Sometimes it is one bubble every few seconds. Be patient. When you find it, keep your thumb on the spot. Dry the tube. Then mark it with the crayon or chalk from the kit. A cross, big enough to find again. Do not skip that. You will lose it otherwise.
