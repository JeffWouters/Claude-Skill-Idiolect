It rained in Wales. I had been told that it would, and I had not believed it, in the way one does not believe any warning about weather that has not yet arrived. It arrived. It came down on the hills and the slate roofs and on me, and it went on coming down with a patience I have never seen in English rain, which always seems to have somewhere else to be.

On the second wet afternoon I stood at the window of the hotel and looked out at nothing in particular, and a servant of the house, perhaps taking pity on me, came and stood beside me and pointed through the rain at a low building across the way. That, he said, was St. David's cell. I asked him who St. David was, not because I did not know, but because I wanted to hear what he would say. He thought about it for some time. St. David, he said at last, was a relation of King Arthur's. I asked him what relation. He did not know. He was a relation, and that seemed to him quite enough, and he went away to attend to something else.

I did not think it quite enough, and when I got home I looked the matter up in a Welsh biographical compendium, a large and learned work which I had supposed would settle it. It did not settle it. It considered, with great seriousness, whether St. David had been Arthur's nephew or Arthur's uncle, and it laid out the case for each, and it declined to choose between them. It then observed, in the same even tone, that there were scholars who held that Arthur was not a man at all but a constellation, and that this view could not be lightly set aside. As to the question of dates, which I had innocently thought might help, the compendium said that chronology was of small account in such matters, and passed on.

I closed the book with a new respect for the hotel servant. He had told me that St. David was some relation of Arthur's, and had not pretended to know more. The learned work had told me the same thing at much greater length, and had added the possibility that one of the two parties was a group of stars. Of the two authorities, I think I prefer the one who stood with me at the window, in the rain, and did not guess.
