Rear wheels are the more awkward ones, because of the chain. Shift onto the smallest cog first, which slackens everything and makes the wheel easier to drop out. Open the quick release or loosen the axle nuts, pull the derailleur back gently, and ease the wheel down and forward out of the frame.

Next, let out whatever air is left and push the tyre bead into the middle of the rim all the way round, which gives you a little slack. Hook one tyre lever under the bead opposite the valve and fold it back against a spoke. Put the second lever in a short distance along and run it round the rim until one side of the tyre comes free. You only need one side off.

Push the valve up through the rim, then pull the inner tube out, working round from the valve.

Finding the hole is the fiddly part. Pump a little air into the tube and pass it slowly through a bowl of water, a section at a time, squeezing gently as you go. Watch for a thin stream of bubbles.
