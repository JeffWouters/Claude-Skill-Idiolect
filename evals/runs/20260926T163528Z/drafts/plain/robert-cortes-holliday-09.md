Nobody who writes for a living can tell you, with any honesty, when the work will come and when it will refuse to. I have stopped pretending that I can.

Some years ago I agreed to write a series of articles for pay. It was meant to be a modest arrangement, a few pieces and a cheque at the end of each. Instead the thing grew under my hands. One article asked for another, and the series turned itself into a book, and the book took hold of me so completely that for a long stretch I thought of nothing else. I worked early and late without being asked, and without much noticing that I was working at all.

Later a commission came along that should have been the easiest job of my life. The subject suited me, the terms were generous, and nothing stood in the way but the writing. It stalled. Weeks became months, and I sent my publishers a steady supply of excuses, each more ingenious than the last and none of them the truth, which was simply that the words would not come. I could not have told them why, because I did not know.

I once shared rooms with a man whose case was stranger still. Whenever he resolved to live sensibly, going to bed at a decent hour, eating plainly, walking in the fresh air and keeping away from drink, his work dried up entirely. He would sit at his desk with a clear head and produce nothing worth keeping. But after a night of excess, when by every rule he ought to have been fit for nothing, he would sit down and write better than he ever did otherwise. I watched this happen more than once, and I never saw it fail. [example needed]

I do not offer his habits as a method. I mention them because they make nonsense of the neat advice we are all given about regular hours and clean living, and because my own experience makes nonsense of it in a different way.

I should like to know whether any psychologist has ever looked into this properly and found an explanation. If one has, I have not come across it. Until someone does, I suspect most of us will go on as we are, grateful when the work arrives, bewildered when it leaves, and inventing excuses in between.
