When the top surface slopes, the log sits at an angle, and the maul meets it glancing rather than square. The blade skids off, and the head keeps travelling, towards your shins or your feet. Before I start I check that the block sits flat on the ground and that its top is flat too, and I fix either before I lift anything.

Stand a log on the block and look at its end before you swing. Most seasoned rounds have drying cracks running out from the centre, and those cracks are the log telling you where it wants to open. Aim along the biggest one.

The swing itself is less about strength than people think. Feet apart, the log straight in front of you, slide your top hand down the handle as the maul comes over and let the weight of the head drop through the log. Keep your eyes on the spot you are aiming for, not on the maul.
