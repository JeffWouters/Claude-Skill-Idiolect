The preparation takes the most time. First I halve the oranges and juice them, keeping every drop. The pips and the pith scraped from inside go into a muslin bag, tied tight. That bag matters more than anything else in the pan, because the pips and pith hold the pectin that makes the marmalade set. Without it you get orange syrup.

Then the peel. I slice it as finely as I can manage, in thin strips, which takes patience and a sharp knife. Thick peel is fine if that is what you like, but it takes longer to soften and it never feels as good in the mouth. Next day it comes up to a gentle simmer, and here the only rule is not to hurry. The peel has to cook until it is completely soft, until a piece squashes easily between finger and thumb. Sugar added before that point hardens the peel, and nothing afterwards will undo it.

Once the peel is soft, I lift out the muslin bag and let it cool enough to handle. Then I squeeze it hard over the pan, pressing out as much of the thick, cloudy liquid as it will give. That liquid is the pectin, and it is what will make everything set.
