The old grout had gone grey at the bottom. Not all of it. The two rows nearest the water, and the corner under the taps. It was soft there. I could press a thumbnail into it and leave a mark. That is the sign. Grout should be hard like stone. It should not give.

I put the job off for a winter. Then a tile by the soap dish rang hollow when I tapped it. So I bought the grout.

Here is what it takes.

A grout rake with a carbide blade. A bag of white grout, five kilos, 11 euros 40. A rubber float. A bucket, a sponge, a clean cloth. Masking tape for the edge of the bath. An old towel in the bottom of it. The rake was my father's. The blade is new.

The towel matters. The first time, I did not use one. A chip of old grout went down the plughole. A tile corner fell and marked the enamel. The mark is still there. Small and grey. A pencil dot.

Raking out is the slow part. You draw the blade along each joint, not across it. Short strokes. Two or three millimetres deep, no more. If you go deeper you loosen the tile. If you go shallower the new grout has nothing to hold. It comes out as dust and small white crumbs. The sound is dry and steady. Scrape, scrape, tap.

I did the bottom three rows and the corner. That is 41 tiles. It took two evenings, about three hours in all. My wrist knew about it on the Wednesday.

Then the hollow tile. It came off with a putty knife and a little care. The adhesive behind it had gone to powder. I scraped the back, buttered it fresh and pressed it in. Tape across it overnight to hold it level.
