It is a plain white loaf with a little wholemeal in it. 500 grams of flour. 350 millilitres of water. 10 grams of salt, 7 of dried yeast. Nothing else. No sugar, no oil.

The flour comes from the mill shop on the ring road. A 25 kilogram sack costs 31 euros. I keep it in a metal bin with a tight lid in the shed. The mice found an open bag once. They do not get a second chance.

Here is the order.

I start at seven. The kitchen is cold at that hour. So I fill the kettle first and warm the bowl with hot water from the tap. A cold bowl slows the yeast. You lose half an hour and do not know why.

Then the water. I mix it from the kettle and the cold tap until it feels like nothing on the inside of my wrist. That is about 37 degrees. I checked it once with a thermometer. I have not needed one since.

The flour goes in the bowl first. That is the rule: salt on one side, yeast on the other. Salt kills yeast if they sit together. My grandmother never measured anything. She always kept those two apart.

I pour in the water and stir with the handle of a wooden spoon. The handle works better than the bowl of the spoon. Less dough sticks to it. Then I tip it all out onto the table.

The kneading takes 10 minutes. I use the clock on the cooker. Push away with the heel of the hand. Fold it back. Turn it a quarter. Push, fold, turn. Push, fold, turn.

At first it is rough and wet and clings to everything. I do not add flour. That was my mistake for the first two years. Extra flour makes a dry, close loaf. You have to keep going until the dough takes up the water on its own.
