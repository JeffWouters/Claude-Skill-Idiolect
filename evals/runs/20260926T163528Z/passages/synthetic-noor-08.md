A plastic bucket. Warm water, about four litres. 200 millilitres of white vinegar. A soft cloth for the frames. A chamois for the sills. A stack of old newspaper, torn into half sheets. The short stepladder from the shed.

I pick a grey day. That is the first rule. Sun dries the glass before you can wipe it, and then you get streaks. Grey and still is best. A little wind is fine. Rain is not.

I start upstairs and work down. Drips run down, never up. If you do the ground floor first, you do it twice.

First the frames. I wipe the sills and the frames with the cloth and plain water. Dead flies. Old spider silk. A grit of dust in the corners. If you skip this, the dirt from the frame ends up on the glass.

So then the glass. I dip the cloth in the vinegar water and wet the whole pane. Not dripping. Just wet. Then I take a half sheet of newspaper and crumple it into a loose ball. About the size of a fist.

You wipe in circles first. Then top to bottom in straight lines. The paper squeaks when the glass is clean. It is a dry, thin sound, like a finger on a balloon. When it stops squeaking, the paper is too wet. It goes in the bag and I take a new one.

The ink does no harm. People expect grey hands and grey glass. The hands, yes. The glass comes up clear. I think the ink helps it polish. I have never proved it.

The outside is the hard part. The upstairs windows open outward on a hinge. From inside I can reach most of the outer pane. Most, not all. The bottom corner of the back bedroom I cannot reach at all. I lean out as far as is sensible and leave a strip about the width of a hand.
