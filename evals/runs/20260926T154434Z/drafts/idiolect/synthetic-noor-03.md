The kitchen tap drips. It has done that for [number needed, how long]. I told myself it was nothing. Then I measured it.

I put a jug under the spout at night. In the morning it held [number needed, how much] millilitres. That is a lot of water for a tap that is off. So I could not ignore it any more. Not once I had the number.

It is an old brass tap. I did not want to scratch it, or break anything behind it. So I did the dull part first. Here is the order.

First I covered the drain. A cloth over the plughole, pushed well in. Small parts go down drains. I did not want to fish one out.

Then I shut off the water. There is a valve under the sink. I turned it until it stopped. Not hard. Just until it stopped.

Then I opened the tap to empty the line. It ran, then it spat, then it went quiet. I left it open.

Only then did I start on the tap itself. The handle came first. [example needed, how the handle is fixed on]. It came off in the end.

Under the handle is the inner assembly. That is the part that does the work. It screws into the body. This is where you can do harm. The body is fixed to the sink. If you turn the insides without holding the body, the whole tap turns with it. Then you have a second job.

So I braced the body with one hand. I turned the insides with a tool in the other. It was tighter than you think. Then it gave.

Tonight the insides lie by the sink, on a clean cloth. The other cloth is still in the drain. The jug is still under the spout. It is dry.
