I bleed the radiators once a year. It never stays done. The air comes back, every winter. It always has.

Here is the job. [number needed] radiators. One radiator key. One old cloth. The heating off and the water cool before I start.

First the top floor. Air rises, so it sits up there. I hold the cloth under the valve, then turn the key slowly. A quarter turn is enough. Then the hiss. That is the air coming out. I wait. Not for long. The hiss stops and water comes. A few drops at first, then a steady bead.

That is the time to close it. Not a second later. And not hard. Shut the valve too tight and you wear the seat. [example needed]. So now I close it until the water stops and no further. Snug is enough.

Then the next one. And the next. Top floor, then down, one room at a time, back towards the boiler.

Letting air out lets the pressure out too. So the last job is at the boiler. I look at the pressure gauge. [number needed]. Too low. The filling hose hangs under the boiler. I open both taps on it, slowly, and watch the needle. It moves slowly. Then faster than you think. I shut the taps at [number needed]. I check the gauge twice. Once straight away. Once when the heat has been on a while.

Then the heat goes back on. And I go round again. Hand flat on each radiator. Top and bottom. Warm all over, evenly, is right. Warm at the bottom and cold at the top means air is still in there. Then I bleed it again.

Every year I think I have got it all out. I have not. Every year some comes back.

This morning I did the round. Every one warm from top to bottom. The key is back by the boiler.
