We keep one list. It is on paper. It hangs on the fridge under a magnet. Not on a phone.

It has three sections. Groceries go at the top. Quick jobs go in the middle. Those are the ones that take [number needed, how many minutes] or less. Big projects go at the bottom. They sit there the longest. Some never leave.

Not everyone may write in every section. [example needed, who may add to each one]. The bottom section is the strictest. Otherwise the list fills up with wishes. Wishes are not jobs.

We did not start with paper. Here is what we tried first.

We tried apps. They worked well for [number needed, how long]. Then nobody opened them. A list you have to go and look for is a list you do not see. So the jobs sat in a phone and did not get done.

Then a notebook. That was better. But it lived in a drawer. Or on a table. Or in a bag. Or in the car. It was never where the person holding the pen was.

Then a whiteboard. It hung on the wall. It was always in sight. But it wiped clean. And when a job was done, we wiped it off. After a while that was the problem. We could see what was left. We could never see what we had done.

So now it is paper. When a job is done, it gets a line through it. Not rubbed out. Struck through. You can still read it. By the end of the week the sheet is half lines. That is the part I like best. It is a small record of work. On a bad day it is proof that something got done.

When the sheet is full, it comes down. A new one goes up.
