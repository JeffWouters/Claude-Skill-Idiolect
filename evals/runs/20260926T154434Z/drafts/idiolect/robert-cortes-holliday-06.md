They left, as I recall, in a manner that was very nearly theatrical.  Arm in arm, the whole company of them, they went out through the door and marched up the street in step, like a small and rather undisciplined regiment returning from some private victory.  Passers-by turned to look.  It was, so to say, an exit.  One half expected a band.

He came back afterward, not once but again and again, and on these later visits he came alone.  I have seldom seen anybody browse with such complete and unguarded absorption.  He would stand before a shelf with his head thrust forward and his eyes brought down to within a few inches of the bindings, peering along the titles one after another, as a child peers into a shop window at Christmas, forgetful of everything else in the world, of the hour, of his hat, of the people who wished to get past him.  Now and then he drew out a volume, turned it over, examined it as though it were a curious shell picked up on a beach, and put it back in precisely the wrong place.  There was nothing of the celebrated novelist about him at such moments.  He was simply a man enjoying himself.

And he always asked for the same assistant.  Why this particular young man, nobody could say; he had been civil on the first occasion, or else he merely had a face that was easy to remember.  In any case, the novelist would come in, look about, and inquire for him by name, and would wait, if necessary, until he was free.

Now the position of a clerk in a shop is a peculiar one, and it is perhaps not generally understood.  He is there to be pleasant.  He is there to be pleasant to everybody: to the customer who knows exactly what he wants and the customer who has no idea, to the customer who is charming and the customer who plainly regards him as something between a machine and a servant.  If he is sneered at, he smiles.  If he is contradicted, he agrees.  If he is treated with open contempt, he says "Certainly, sir," and goes to fetch the book.  That is the job.  It is not, when you come to consider it, an easy one; and it is not a thing one learns from any manual.
