I found a hole in the heel. Not a big one. Wool socks go there first. [example needed]

My grandfather had a darning tool. [description of the tool needed] It sat in a drawer for years after he died. Nobody knew what it was. I did. I had watched him use it, though he never showed me how. So I taught myself. Slowly. Badly at first. It took me [number needed] socks to get one I could wear.

It goes like this. Slip the tool inside the sock, under the hole. Stretch the wool over it. Not tight. Just flat. Then run stitches across the hole, side to side, well past the edges into the good wool. Those are the long threads. Then turn the sock and weave across them. Over one, under one. Back the other way. Under one, over one. Push each row up against the last. It fills in like a small piece of cloth.

Tension is the thing. That is where I went wrong at the start. I pulled every thread tight. The patch looked neat. It felt like a stone. A hard little ridge right under the heel. After an hour of walking I had a sore foot. So now I keep it loose. Looser than looks right. The patch should give, like the wool around it. If you press it and it does not move, it is too tight. Unpick it. Start again.

People ask why I bother. Socks are cheap. That is true. But I do not think of the time as lost. I sit down with the sock and the tool and a lamp. I do not look at a screen. I just weave. It is quiet work. I get up calmer than I sat down.

And the heel ends up stronger than it was. The patch is thicker than the knit. It wears slower. The sock usually goes somewhere else next. The toe, most often. That is fine. I know how to do toes now too.
