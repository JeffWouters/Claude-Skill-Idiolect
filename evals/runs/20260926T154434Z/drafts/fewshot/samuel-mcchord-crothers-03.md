The night was dark, and the preacher, if we may still call him so, was making the best time he could along the road that led away from the town. He did not look back. He had reasons for not looking back, and they were good reasons. But as the distance grew between him and his late congregation, his mind, relieved of immediate anxiety, turned to a more leisurely inquiry. Had he done wrong?

It was a question he was well fitted to answer. He had spent a good many evenings of late expounding the moral law to others, and he saw no reason why he should not apply it to himself. He was not a man to shirk an examination of conscience, provided he might conduct it himself.

He went over the ground carefully. He had represented himself as something he was not. That was undeniable. But what, after all, had he taken? He had taken his meals and his lodging, and such small contributions as the people saw fit to give. He had not grown rich. He was leaving with very little more than he had brought. When he put the matter to himself in this way, it did not look so very black.

There was, it must be confessed, one thing that troubled him. It was not anything that had happened in the town. It was an old affair, and it had to do with a farmer and some honey. He had taken the honey. He had not asked for it, and he had not paid for it. He had been young then, and the honey had been very good, but he did not try to excuse himself on either ground. That had been theft, plain theft, and he owned it. It was a relief to have found something that he could honestly repent of. It gave a certain solidity to the rest of his reasoning.

But a fraud? No, he would not accept the word. A fraud, as he understood it, was a man who deceived others in order to enrich himself. He had never looked for profit. He had sought only subsistence, the bare means of keeping soul and body together. Was a man to be condemned because he wished to eat? The birds were fed, and nobody called them impostors.

By the time the sky began to lighten, the argument was complete. He had weighed himself in the balance and had not been found wanting, except in the matter of the honey. And the honey was a long way behind him.
