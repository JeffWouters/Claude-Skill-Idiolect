Then the closing. This is the part people rush. I do not. I hold a rag under the valve and I wait. First comes the air. A thin hiss, steady and dry. Then it starts to spit. Then water comes. A clean run of it, no bubbles, no sputter. That is when I close it. Not before. Not after the floor is wet.

Close it gently. A small turn is enough. Do not lean on the key. The valve is small and it is soft. If you force it shut, you damage the seat. Then it weeps all winter and you are back with the rag. I found that out the hard way. [example needed]

So I go round the house like that. Every radiator. Key, rag, wait, water, close. It is slow. It is meant to be slow.

Then the boiler. Letting out air lets out pressure too. Always. So when the last valve is shut, I go and look at the gauge. It has dropped. It always drops. The needle should sit at [pressure figure needed]. Mine sat well below it.

The filling hose is the short loop under the boiler. Two small taps, one at each end. I open the first. Then the second, slowly. You hear the water go in. I watch the needle, not the taps. It climbs faster than you think. When it reaches the mark, I close both taps. Then I check the needle again a minute later. If it has crept past the mark, I let a little out at a radiator. If it has fallen, there is still air somewhere.

Last, the walk round. I turn the heating up and give it time. Then I put a hand on each radiator. Top and bottom. Left end and right end. They should all be warm all over. A cold top means air. A cold patch low down is something else.

This year they were all warm. Every one. That will not last. The air comes back. It comes back every year. So next autumn I will get the key and the rag out again.
