It may be objected that it is not quite the thing for one who has been a clerk in a bookshop to go about telling what he saw there.  I have thought of that.  And I have concluded that I am in excellent company.  The coach drivers of the great houses have always known a good deal about the great, and it has not, so far as I have heard, been thought wrong in them to mention it.  The footman stands behind the chair; he has ears.  Much of what we know of the manners of other days we know, when you come to think of it, from people who were paid to hold the door.  And there was the waiter in Rome.  He was the most candid man I ever met.  He told me the private habits of half the notable visitors to the city, and he told it without malice and without the least embarrassment.  It was, he seemed to feel, a part of his employment.  I think it was.

So I shall go on.

It was a morning like any other.  There was a gentleman waiting in the shop.  He was very well dressed; better dressed, I recall thinking, than the occasion required, if there was an occasion.  He was not looking at books.  He stood near the door with his hands behind him and the air of a man who has been told to wait and has agreed to wait, but who would not, himself, have chosen to wait just there.  He was, as it turned out, the husband.  I did not know that then.  I knew only that he was waiting, and that he did it well.

Then the novelist came in.  I have seen a good many portraits of [the novelist] since, and not one of them is like what came in at the door.  He was stout.  He wore a hat which I shall not attempt to describe, except to say that it was not the hat one would have expected, and that I have never seen another like it.  He took it off, and he was bald--entirely, shiningly bald.  And before the hat was fairly off he was talking.  He talked to the husband, and to the shop, and to me.  There was no preliminary.  He simply began, as though he had been talking all the way from wherever he had come, and had merely stepped through the door in the middle of a sentence.
