The list is on the fridge. It is a sheet of paper under a magnet. A pencil hangs next to it on a string. That is the whole system. It has lasted longer than anything else we have tried.

The sheet has three parts. Groceries go at the top. Milk, bread, whatever has run out. Quick jobs go in the middle. Anything under half an hour. Change a bulb. Post a letter. Oil the gate. Big projects go at the bottom. Those are the ones that take a weekend, or several. There are only ever a few of them. Anyone in the house can add to the top two parts. The bottom part is different. A big project only goes on when [who decides needed]. That rule keeps the bottom part honest. Otherwise it fills up with wishes.

We did not start with paper. We started with apps. We tried [number] of them. They all failed the same way. The list was in a phone, and the phone was in a pocket, and nobody looked. Then a notebook. The notebook failed because it had pages. Old jobs got buried on page four. Nobody turned back. Then a whiteboard. That one nearly worked. It was on the wall. Everyone could see it. But it wiped clean. A sleeve would rub out half a line. A job would vanish and nobody knew if it was done. [example needed]

So now it is paper. And when a job is done, it gets a line through it. Not rubbed out. Struck through, once, so you can still read it. That matters more than I expected. At the end of the week the sheet is a mess of crossed-out lines. It shows what got done, and by whom, if you know the handwriting. It shows what did not. When the sheet is full I copy the open jobs onto a new one. The old one I keep.
