I leave the house at the same time every morning. [time needed] Not a minute earlier. The walk to the station takes [time needed]. I have timed it. Many times. In the rain, in the dark, with a bag, without one. It hardly changes.

That leaves me a margin. A small one. Enough to stand on the platform for a minute or two. Not enough to get bored. If I leave too early, I stand in the cold. If I leave too late, I run. I do not run. So I leave at the same time.

The same people are there. Most mornings. I know their faces. I know their coats. I know who stands by the bench and who stands at the far end, where the doors open. We do not talk. Nobody talks. A nod, sometimes. Not always. I could not tell you a single name. But I notice when one of them is missing. I expect they notice when I am.

Once the train did not come. There was an announcement. Then another. A delay of [length of delay needed]. I thought someone would complain. Nobody did. Nobody left either. We all just stood there and waited. Some read. Some looked at the rails. It was oddly calm. When the train finally came in, we all got on as if nothing had happened. [example needed]

Then the seat. I have a preferred one. It depends on the season. In winter I want the side the sun comes in. It is warm through the glass. In summer I want the other side. The shade side. Otherwise I cook for the whole ride. [which side and which carriage needed]

I do not always get it. Someone else is sometimes in it. That is fine. I take the next best. But I get it most days. I think the others have their seats too. We have sorted it out without a word.

It is a small routine. The same door, the same walk, the same faces. I like it more than I would admit.
