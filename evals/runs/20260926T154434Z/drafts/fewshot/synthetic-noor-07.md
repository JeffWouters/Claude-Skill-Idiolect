The washer was the whole problem. A small rubber ring. It had worn out. That is why the tap dripped. Water got past it every time I turned it off. It took me longer to find that out than to fix it.

I took the old one to the hardware shop down the road. I always take the old part. It saves guessing. The man at the counter found a match in a drawer. It cost [price needed]. Almost nothing. So I bought a bag of them. And a spare of the other ring, the thin one on the spindle. They cost [price needed] as well.

Most of the job was the getting ready. That is always true with taps. Turn off the water under the sink. Open the tap to drain what is left in the pipe. Put the plug in so nothing small goes down it. Lay a towel in the sink. Lay the parts out on it in order, as they come off. That took longer than the fix itself. I did not mind. It is the part that stops you making a mess.

The new washer went on in a moment. Then the reassembly. Slowly. Each part back in the order it came off. The towel helps here. I did not tighten anything hard. Snug is enough. Brass is soft. If you crank it down, you crush the new rubber and you are back where you started.

Then the water. Not all at once. I opened the valve under the sink a little at a time. You hear the pipe fill. There is a knock, then a hiss, then quiet. I left the tap open until the water ran clean and smooth. No spitting. Then I closed it. And I watched it.

Nothing. No drip. I watched it for a while longer to be sure. Still nothing.

The rest of the bag is in the drawer by the sink. With a note of which tap it fits. The next one will not take a trip to the shop.
