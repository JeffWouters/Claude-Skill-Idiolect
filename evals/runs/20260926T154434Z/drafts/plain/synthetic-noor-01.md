We never threw the old to-do sheets away. Each one came off the fridge when it was full or too stained to read, and went into a box instead of the bin. Years of them are in there now. I started rereading them expecting a pile of chores. What I found was closer to a diary, written by a family that thought it was only making lists.

The first thing that jumps out is the rhythm. The same chores come back at the same point every year, as reliably as the seasons that cause them: [example needed] in spring, [example needed] before winter. Laid side by side, the sheets show a household that runs on a calendar nobody ever wrote down. We simply did the same things when the same time came around, and the lists remembered it for us.

Then there are the tasks that would not die. Some items were copied from one sheet to the next, month after month, sometimes year after year. [example needed] is the worst offender. You can see the handwriting change as different people carried it forward, each one sure they would get to it soon. Others did the opposite. They appear once or twice, and then they are simply gone. No line through them, no note, no sign they were ever done. Whatever happened to them, the sheets are not telling.

The grocery entries are the part I did not expect to find moving. Read in order, they trace the children growing up. Early sheets ask for [example needed]. Later ones change as tastes and appetites change, and the quantities creep upward. And then, sheet by sheet, the items that were only ever for them start to drop off. Nobody decided to stop buying them. The children left, and the list quietly noticed before we did.

It is a strange record to keep by accident. I am glad we kept it.
