Bleeding the radiators is one of those jobs that comes round every year, and every year I half forget how it ends. Starting is easy. You turn the little valve, you hear the hiss, and you feel as though you are doing something useful. Finishing it properly is where the care goes.

The first thing to get right is knowing when to close each valve. The hiss of air is the signal to wait. When it stops and water starts to come through, even a thin bead of it, that is the moment. Close it then, and close it gently. The valve is small and it does not need force. Turning it hard to be sure only risks damaging the seat or the thread, and a valve that no longer shuts cleanly is a much bigger problem than a bit of air. A cloth underneath catches the drips; a steady hand does the rest.

Once every radiator has been done, the boiler pressure will have dropped, because the air you let out was taking up space the water now has to fill. So the next step is the filling hose. Open it slowly, watch the gauge, and stop when the needle reaches the right level for your system [pressure figure needed]. Then close it again, fully. It is easy to overshoot if you look away, so I do not look away.

The last part is the one people skip. After the heating has run for a while, go round the house and put a hand on every heater. Each one should feel warm across its whole surface, top to bottom, end to end. A cold band along the top usually means there is still air in it, and that radiator gets another turn.

Even when it all goes well, I know I will be back next year. Trapped air returns, slowly and quietly, whatever you do. That is not a failure of the job. It is simply the rhythm of it, and finishing well each time keeps the rhythm easy.
