He had, it became clear, only the vaguest idea of how a shop actually works. In his mind there seemed to be one salesman, and that one salesman did everything. The same man who greeted him at the door would presumably also find the books, wrap them, write up the order, take the money, arrange the post, keep the accounts and answer the letters. The notion that a firm might divide its work among different people, each with a separate job and a separate desk, does not appear to have occurred to him. He spoke to whoever was in front of him as though that person were the whole business.

This was charming in its way, but it made the simplest transaction surprisingly hard. On one visit he decided he wanted copies of his own works sent to a relative. That part was straightforward enough; the books were on the shelves and the order could be written down. Then he thanked everyone, gathered himself together and left. It was only after the door had closed that anyone realised he had not said who the relative was. There was no name. There was no address. There was an order for a parcel with nowhere to send it.

When he came back, the missing details had to be drawn out of him one at a time. What was her name? He told us, and then looked slightly surprised that we had needed it. Where did she live? He had to think. Which street, which town, which number? Each answer came only after a question, and each question seemed to strike him as a small revelation, as if he had assumed the information would somehow travel from his head to the parcel without passing through anyone else. It was not that he was unwilling. He simply had not imagined that the salesman, the one who did everything, did not already know.

And at every step he apologised. He was sorry for the trouble, sorry for forgetting, sorry for taking up our time. The apologies were sincere, and they made it impossible to be irritated with him. A man who writes whole books full of carefully arranged detail, and who cannot remember to give a shop a relative's address, is a contradiction, but it is a very human one.
