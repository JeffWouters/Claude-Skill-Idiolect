I keep the old to-do sheets. All of them. Not on purpose, at first. They went into a box when they were done, and the box never went out.

There are [number needed: how many] years of them now. I sat down with the box and read them in order. That took longer than I meant it to.

The chores come round like weather. Clear the gutters. Service the boiler. Put the winter coats away, then get them out again. The same lines appear in the same months, year after year, in slightly different handwriting. You could date a sheet by its chores alone. I tried it with a few. I was right more often than not.

Then there are the tasks that never got done. They move from sheet to sheet. Fix the shed door. Sort the loft. One of them, [example needed: a long-postponed task], turns up on sheet after sheet for years. Then it stops. It is not crossed out. It is just not there on the next sheet. I do not know if we did it or gave up on it. Nobody remembers.

Some tasks vanished with no explanation at all. One week they are there. The next they are gone. I would like to say we finished them. I doubt it.

The grocery lines are the part I did not expect. You can watch the children grow up in them. First the small things. [example needed: an early grocery entry for young children.] Then more food, and more of it. Then their own requests, in their own writing, squeezed in at the bottom. Then, one by one, those lines thin out. The quantities drop. The requests stop. The list gets shorter, and it stays short.

So the box is not really a box of chores. It is a record of who lived here, and when.

This morning I put the sheets back in order. The latest one went on top. It is a short one.
