Where do our deadlines come from? You might assume, if you have spent any time working towards one, that it was handed down from somewhere beyond the team's control. It may be that far more of them are chosen internally than we tend to admit.

This is not in itself a criticism. A date chosen by a team or its managers can serve a real purpose, and many are set with good intentions. [example needed: a due date set internally and later treated as external]. The difficulty is that once a date has been announced, its origin is quickly forgotten, and it begins to carry the weight of an outside constraint whether or not it ever was one.

There is also the effect such dates have on the people working towards them. An approaching cutoff concentrates the mind; I suspect most of us have done some of our clearest work in the days before one. But the same pressure invites shortcuts, and decisions taken quickly because there is no longer time to take them well. It invites overwork too, and the cost of that is borne quietly, in evenings and weekends, rather than recorded anywhere a project plan would notice.

It seems to me that the striking thing is not that these effects exist but that nobody weighs them. Focus is gained and quality is put at risk, yet the exchange is rarely made explicitly by anyone with the standing to make it. The date simply arrives, and the trade-off happens underneath it, unexamined. Is it any wonder that teams feel the consequences of a choice they never knew was being made?

A due date is, in the end, one of the few decisions in working life whose costs are almost entirely deferred to the people who did not make it. Perhaps that is why it is so easy to set one, and so hard to set one well.
