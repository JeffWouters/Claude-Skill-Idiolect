The kitchen tap drips. It has dripped for a long time. I had got used to it.

Then I measured it. I put a jug under the spout and left it. [number needed: how much water, over how long.] That was the end of ignoring it. A drip is easy to live with. A jugful is not.

It is an old brass tap. So I did not want to force it. Old brass does not forgive much.

Here is how I started.

First the drain. I put the plug in and laid a cloth over it. Small parts come out of a tap, and small parts go down plugholes.

Then the water. I shut off the supply under the sink. The valve was stiff, but it turned.

Then I emptied the line. I opened the tap and let it run until it stopped. It spat a bit. Then nothing. If water still comes out after that, the supply is not really off. Go back and check it.

So now the tap was dry, and I could take it apart.

The handle came first. [example needed: how the handle is fixed, e.g. a screw under a cap.] It came away with some coaxing.

Then the inner assembly. This is the part that does the work, and the part most likely to be the problem. It unscrews from the body. The trick is to hold the body still while you turn. If you do not, all the force goes into the tap body and the pipe under it. That is how old taps crack. So I braced the body with one hand, or with a second spanner, and turned the assembly with the other. It was tighter than you think. Then it gave.

This morning the parts are laid out on the cloth in the order they came off. The tap is open to the air. It is not dripping.
