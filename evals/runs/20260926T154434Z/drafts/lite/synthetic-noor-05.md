I bleed the radiators every autumn. Not because I remember. Because the pipes start to tick.

So this year I did the whole house in one go. [number needed: how many radiators] radiators, top floor first. Here is how it goes.

First I turn the heating off and let everything cool. Hot water spits. Then I fold an old cloth under the valve and fit the key. A quarter turn is enough. You hear the hiss straight away. Air, then more air, then a sputter.

The trick is knowing when to stop. You close the valve when water comes, not before and not long after. A thin steady bead. That is the signal. If you keep going you get a lap full of black water. If you close it on the sputter you leave air behind. And if you force the key shut you can strip the valve. [example needed: a valve that was damaged]. So I turn it back gently. Snug, not tight.

Then the boiler. Letting the air out lets the pressure down. So after the last radiator I go down and look at the gauge. It had dropped. I opened the filling hose under the boiler. Slowly. A little at a time, watching the needle climb. Then I closed it and checked again. The needle stayed put.

Then the heating back on, and a walk round the house. Hand flat on every radiator. Top and bottom. Warm all over is what you want. Cold at the top means air is still in there. So back to that one with the key and the cloth.

They were all warm this time. They will not stay that way. The air comes back every year. I do not know where from. I have stopped asking.

Tonight the house is quiet. No ticking. The key is on the shelf above the boiler, where it lives. The cloth is on the last radiator, drying.
