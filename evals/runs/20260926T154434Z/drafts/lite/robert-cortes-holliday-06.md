They went out, when they did go, in a manner which the shop did not soon forget.  The whole party of them linked arms at the door, the novelist somewhere in the middle, and off they went up the street in step, marching, as it were, to a band that nobody else could hear.  The people on the pavement made way.  The clerks stood at the window and watched them go.  It was not dignified.  It was a good deal better than dignified.

He came back, of course.  He came back a number of times after that, alone, and on these later visits there was nothing of the parade about him.  He browsed.  Now, a great many people browse in a bookshop, or say they do; they drift along the tables with one eye on the clock and the other on the door.  That was not his way.  He browsed as a small boy looks into a toy-shop window, entirely, with the whole of his attention and none of it left over for anything else in the world.  He would stoop until his nose was all but upon the shelf, and peer along the backs of the books one by one, and take down a volume, and put it back, and take down the next.  You could have spoken to him and he would not have heard you.  I do not think he knew he was in a shop at all.

And when at last he came up for air he would ask for the same assistant.  Always the same one.  If that particular young man was busy he would wait; if he was out to lunch he would wait longer.

Consider for a moment the lot of a shop assistant.  His business, from the first of the morning to the closing of the doors, is to be civil.  He is civil to the lady who wants a book whose title she has forgotten, by an author whose name she never knew, with a blue cover.  He is civil to the gentleman who informs him that the whole establishment is run by fools.  Whatever scorn a customer cares to pour upon him, and customers are free with it, he receives with a bow and a "Yes, sir," and is back at his post the next morning to receive some more.  It is a trade in patience.  Nobody much remarks it.

So it was something, in that position, to be asked for by name.
