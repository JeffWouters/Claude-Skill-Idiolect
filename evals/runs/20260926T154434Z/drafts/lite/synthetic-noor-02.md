We run the house from one sheet of paper on the fridge. Not an app. A sheet of paper, held on with a magnet.

It has three sections. Groceries at the top. Quick tasks in the middle. Large projects at the bottom. The quick tasks are the ones you can do in [number needed: how many] minutes or less. Change a bulb. Post a letter. The large projects are the ones that need a weekend, or a tradesman, or both.

Here are the rules. Anyone in the house can add to the groceries. Anyone can add a quick task. The large projects are different. [example needed: who may add a large project, and why.] That rule sounds fussy. It is the one that keeps the list honest.

It took us a while to get here. First we tried apps. They were good at reminding us and bad at being looked at. Nobody opens an app to see what needs doing. They open it when it pings, and then they swipe it away. [example needed: a specific app failure.]

Then a notebook. That was better, for a while. But a notebook shuts. It goes in a drawer. It goes in a bag and leaves the house. A list you cannot see is a list you do not read.

Then a whiteboard. I had high hopes for the whiteboard. It was always in view. But anyone could wipe anything off it, and things did get wiped. Some were done. Some were not. We could never tell which.

So paper. On the fridge, where everybody looks, whether they mean to or not.

When something is done, it gets a line through it. Not rubbed out. Struck through, so you can still read it. That matters more than you would expect. At the end of the week the sheet shows what we got done, not just what is left. And the large projects, which move slowly, get their line through them in the end too.

Tonight the middle section is mostly lines through words. The groceries are not. The magnet is holding it all up, a little crooked.
