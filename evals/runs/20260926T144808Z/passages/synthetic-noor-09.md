There is a hiss. Air coming out. It smells of metal and old water. The hiss can go on for a while. On the back bedroom one it went on for almost a minute.

And then the sound changes. The hiss goes wet. A spit. Then a bead of water. That is the moment. Turn the key back, clockwise, snug. Not tight. You can strip the little valve if you force it.

Wipe the water. Check the cloth. Move on.

Eight of the nine had some air. The one in the hall had none. It never does. I do not know why. It is the lowest one in the house and the closest to the boiler. Something to do with that.

When they are all done, I go to the boiler. There is a pressure gauge on the front. A round dial with a needle. It should sit between 1 and 1.5 bar when cold. After bleeding it drops, because you let water out. This year it was down to 0.6.

So I top it up. Under the boiler there is a short silver hose with two taps. The filling loop. I open both taps, slowly, and watch the needle climb. At 1.2 I close them. The pipes groan while the new water finds its way round.

Then the heating goes back on.

I walk round the house and feel each radiator again. Top to bottom. Hot all over. The back bedroom one too. It is the best part of the job. You put your hand flat on the metal and the heat is even from edge to edge.

That night the house was quiet. No knocks. No tick. Just the low hum of the pump in the cupboard and the click as the thermostat turned it off.

I will do the same next October. The air comes back. It always does. Small bubbles, a little at a time, over the year.
