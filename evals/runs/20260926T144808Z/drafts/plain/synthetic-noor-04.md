Every autumn, when the heating comes back on after the summer, the pipes start to make noise. Gurgling, knocking, the occasional trickling sound behind the walls. By now I recognise it as the signal that it is time for an annual job: releasing the air that has become trapped in the radiators.

Air in a radiator gets in the way of the hot water. It collects at the top, and the result is a radiator that is only partly doing its job. The easiest way to detect it is by touch. With the heating running, feel along each radiator from bottom to top. If the lower part is warm but the upper sections are noticeably cooler, there is air trapped inside. Going round the house this way tells me which radiators need attention and which can be left alone.

Before opening anything, I switch the system off. This is important. If the pump is still running, it keeps moving water and air around, which makes the job less effective, and a hot system means hot water coming out of the valve. It is better to let everything settle first. [example needed: how long to wait after switching off]

Then I work through the house in a set order, starting on the ground floor and moving upward. Beginning at the bottom makes sense because air rises through the system, so doing the upper floors last gives it the best chance to be caught where it ends up.

At each radiator I open the bleed valve gently. There is no need to force it or open it wide. A small turn is enough to let the air start escaping with a hiss. Going slowly keeps things under control, and it means I can react quickly when the hissing changes, which is the sign that the air is nearly out. I keep a cloth close by to catch any drips. Radiator by radiator, floor by floor, the house gets a little quieter as I go.
