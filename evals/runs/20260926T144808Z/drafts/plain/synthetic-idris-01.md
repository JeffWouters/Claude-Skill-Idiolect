Every so often someone suggests scrapping the standing team meetings altogether. The weekly sync, the Monday check-in, the Thursday review: they fill the calendar, they eat into focus time, and on a bad week it is hard to point to a single decision any of them produced. The case for clearing them out sounds obvious.

I think it is only half right. Judging a standing meeting purely by the decisions it produces misses what many of them are actually for. A regular slot where the same people see each other, hear what the others are working on and notice who seems stretched is a kind of social glue. It keeps contact going between people who would otherwise only meet when something has gone wrong. That contact is hard to measure and easy to underrate, right up until it is gone and a team discovers it has become a set of individuals who share a ticket queue.

That does not mean every standing meeting deserves to live forever. The problem with recurring meetings is that they recur by default. Nobody has to argue for them again once they are on the calendar, so they outlast the reasons they were created. The fix is not to abolish them but to make them earn their place. Every so often, a team should put each standing meeting back on the table and ask plainly: what is this for now, who needs to be here, and would we create it today if it did not exist?

Asked honestly, that question produces three kinds of answer. Some meetings would end, and should, because the problem they were set up to handle is solved or has moved elsewhere. Many more would survive in a different shape: shorter, with fewer people, with a sharper focus, or explicitly relabelled as a chance to stay in touch rather than a decision forum pretending to be one. A few would carry on exactly as they are, and the team would at least know why.

So before scrapping the lot, audit them. Keep the glue, cut the habit, and give every meeting a regular moment where it has to justify itself again. [example needed]
