My morning commute by rail is a routine I have kept for a long time, long enough that most of it now runs without much thought. It starts before I even leave the house. I time my departure carefully, not to the last second, but with a small buffer built in. Enough to absorb a slow traffic light or a missing key, not so much that I end up standing on the platform with nothing to do. Getting that margin right took some trial and error, and now I rarely think about it. I just leave when it is time to leave.

On the platform and on the train, there are faces I know well. We have shared the same carriage for years, often the same end of it, and yet we never talk. Not a word. There is a kind of silent familiarity in that. I know who will be standing near the doors and who will take the seat by the window. I notice when someone is missing, and I suspect they notice when I am. We nod sometimes, or we do not. Nobody seems to feel the need for more, and there is something comfortable about that unspoken arrangement. It is company without obligation.

I half expected people to give up, to get off, make phone calls, find another way in. That is not what happened. Everyone waited. Patiently, without complaint, the same quiet as every other morning. Nobody left.

I still think about that sometimes. It said something about the people who make this journey every day. Perhaps when you have built your mornings around a routine, you learn to trust it, even on the day it lets you down. Or perhaps we simply knew that waiting together was easier than going it alone.
