The young man who took to preaching under an assumed name had no particular preparation for the pulpit. He had, it is true, a good memory and a certain facility of manner, but these are qualities which serve equally well in several other professions. On the evening before his first appearance he was, by his own account, extremely nervous. He had undertaken to instruct a congregation in matters about which he knew very little, and he was not at all sure that the congregation would fail to notice it.

But the event surprised him. When he rose to speak, the words came without difficulty, and they continued to come until he was almost alarmed at their abundance. The congregation listened attentively. Before he sat down he had arrived at the comfortable conclusion that he had been called to the ministry, and that the name under which he had been called was a mere technicality.

A parish in the neighbourhood soon heard of the promising young preacher and engaged his services. He was particularly successful at funerals. There is a kind of eloquence which is wasted upon the living and reserved for the dead, and he possessed it in a remarkable degree; his funeral addresses became so popular that people who had no connection with the deceased attended for the pleasure of hearing him. It was during this prosperous period that he made the acquaintance of a counterfeiter. Unfortunately he had an enemy, and enemies have a habit of inquiring into names. The enemy discovered who the preacher really was and took care that everybody else discovered it too. The effect upon the townsfolk, who were a hot-tempered people at the best of times, was immediate and violent. The whole community fell into a turmoil. Those who had wept at his funerals now wished to arrange one.
