Of all the customers who come to a bookstore, the most exacting is the one who is buying a present.  He does not want a book for himself; he wants a book for somebody else, and he has, apparently, no notion whatever of what that somebody else might like.  He relies upon you.  You suggest a novel.  No, not a novel.  You suggest a volume of essays.  He turns it over, reads the title twice, and puts it down with the expression of a man who has been offered something slightly unwholesome.  You suggest travel, biography, verse, a handsome illustrated edition of something standard.  Each suggestion is considered, courteously, and each is rejected.  In the end he buys, very often, the first thing you showed him.  Or he goes away to think it over, which comes to the same thing for the store, only less profitably.

The difficulty of the salesman's position is not that any single question is hard.  It is that the questions come one after another, without warning, from opposite ends of human interest.  One moment a gentleman wishes to discuss the merits of rival editions of a learned work, and you are expected to have an opinion, and a sound one.  The next moment a lady wants to know whether you have "anything nice" for a girl of a certain age, and she will not tell you the age.  You must change your mind, so to speak, as a man changes trains, quickly and without looking back, and you must never let the one customer perceive that you were, a moment ago, attending gravely to the other.

Consider, if you will, what is asked for in the course of an ordinary day.  A guide to the care of babies.  A prayer book.  [examples needed: further odd requests]  And so on, through every department of life, from the cradle, as it were, to the church door.  Some of these requests are perfectly reasonable.  Some are a little strange.  A few of them are of such a nature that no bookstore in the world could satisfy them, though the customer is quite confident that yours should.

Well, you do what you can.  You find the prayer book.  You find the book about babies.  And you wait, with what patience you possess, for the gentleman who is buying a present.
