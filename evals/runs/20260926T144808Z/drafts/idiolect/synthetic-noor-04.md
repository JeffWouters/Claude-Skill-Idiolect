The pipes start to knock every autumn. Not all at once. A tick here, a gurgle there. Then a real bang in the night.

That is the signal. There is air in the radiators. So once a year, I bleed them.

Here is how I go about it.

First the test. I wait until the heating has been on for a while. Then I go round the house with my hand flat on each radiator. Bottom first. Then the top. A radiator with air in it is warm at the bottom and cool at the top. You can feel the line where the warm stops. If the top is cool, there is air. If it is warm all over, leave it.

I make a list as I go. [number needed, how many radiators need it most years].

Then the switch. I turn the heating off. Not down. Off. And I let it cool. Hot water under pressure is not something to open a valve on. This is the step I am most tempted to skip. I do not skip it.

Then the order. Ground floor first. Then upstairs. I start with the radiator on the list that is lowest in the house and work up from there. [example needed, why this order works in this house].

At each radiator I hold a cloth under the small valve at the top corner. I fit the key. Then I open it. Gently. A quarter turn is plenty. Not a full turn. If you open it hard, it spits. You want a slow hiss. Then you wait.

The hiss is the air leaving. It can take a little while. I stand there with the cloth and listen.

So that is the first half of the job. Knock, feel, switch off, and work up the house one valve at a time.

Tonight the house is quiet. The heating is off. There is a cloth on the landing and the key is in my pocket.
