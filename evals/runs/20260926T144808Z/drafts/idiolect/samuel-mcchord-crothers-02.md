The prospector has a philosophy of his own, and it is a cheerful one. He has lived through the boom and he has lived through the bust, and he has come to regard both with the same good-humoured tolerance. When the town that was to have been a metropolis dwindles to a saloon and a signpost, he does not complain of the injustice of Providence. He treats the whole affair as a practical joke of which he happens to be the victim, and he enjoys it almost as much as if he had played it on somebody else.

What he does not do is learn anything from it. This is the part of his philosophy that most disturbs the moralist. Experience, we are told, is a hard teacher, but the prospector has discovered that it is a teacher whose lessons need not be taken too seriously. He goes out to the next excitement with precisely the same confidence with which he went out to the last. He has been fooled before, and he fully expects to be fooled again. But he would not for the world miss the pleasure of being fooled.

Nor is he altogether wrong. The lore of the mining camps is full of stories in which the expected thing did not happen and the unexpected thing did. The claim that the experts condemned turned out to be the richest in the district. The men who named their holdings in a spirit of gloomy irony, as though to forestall disappointment, found that the most dismal names concealed the greatest wealth. Prudence is a virtue in a settled community, but on the frontier it is merely a way of missing the next strike.

A word should be said about the prospector's drink, since he himself would say a good deal about it. He believes that liquor quickens his wit, and in the evening his conversation certainly seems to him more brilliant than in the morning. But the men of science take a less generous view. They tell us that alcohol does not sharpen the wit at all; it only dulls the faculty of self-criticism, so that a man ceases to notice how dull he has become. The improvement, in other words, is entirely in the audience, and the audience is himself.
