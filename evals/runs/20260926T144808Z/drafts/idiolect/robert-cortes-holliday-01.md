He came in out of the street rather quickly, as a man does who has determined to get a disagreeable business over with, and he came straight to my counter.  He wished, he said, to order a book.  He named it.  I wrote it down.  So far the transaction was of the ordinary character that repeats itself a great many times a day in any bookstore, and I was not, I confess, prepared for anything remarkable.

But it presently appeared that in his view I was the entire establishment.  I was the stock room and the shipping department; I was the bookkeeper, the cashier and the person who ties the parcels; I was, as it were, the firm.  Would I see that the book went off immediately?  Would I attend to the wrapping personally?  Would I make certain that it was charged correctly?  I said that I would.  It seemed simpler.  There is no profit in explaining the division of labour in a large commercial house to a distinguished man of letters who has come in to purchase one book.

Then we arrived at the recipient.  "Now, the name," I said, my pencil waiting.

He gave a name.  Then he withdrew it.  There was, it seemed, a question of initials.  He looked at the ceiling (a thing customers frequently do in a bookstore, as though the ceiling were a directory) and he looked at the floor.  He drummed a little with his fingers upon the counter.  The address came harder still.  He had it in his head, he explained, in a general way; it was the street he was uncertain of, and the number, and to some extent the town.  We laboured at it together, slowly, patiently, earnestly, as two gentlemen might labour over a puzzle in the Sunday newspaper.  In the end there was something written on my slip that I believed the post office would understand.

He thanked me.  He thanked me very courteously indeed, and he evidently meant it.  He drew on his gloves with the air of a man whose afternoon has been relieved of a considerable weight, so to speak, and he went out into the street again.  I have observed people leaving a dentist's chair looking much the same.

The book, I believe, arrived.
