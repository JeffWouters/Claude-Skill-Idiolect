I take the same train every morning. I have done so for years. [number needed, how many years].

Here is the routine.

First the clock. I leave the house at a set time. Not the last possible minute. A few minutes before it. [number needed, the size of the gap]. That small gap is the whole point. It covers a lost key, a slow road, a queue at the ticket machine. Without it I run. With it I walk. I do not like to run for a train.

Then the platform. I stand in the same spot each day. So do the others. There is a group of us. I do not know their names. They do not know mine. We have never spoken. Not once.

And yet I know them. Their coats, their bags, the way each of them waits. [example needed, one of the regulars and a detail about them]. If one of them is missing, I notice. I think they notice too. Nobody says so. That is the rule.

So we stand, and we board, and we sit in much the same seats. It is a quiet sort of company. It asks for nothing.

Once, the train was badly late. [example needed, the cause and how long it lasted]. The board kept changing. Then it went blank. I thought people would give up. Leave and find a bus or a taxi. That is what I would have guessed.

They did not. Nobody left. We all stood there and waited. No fuss. No sighs worth hearing. Just the same silent group on the platform. When the train came at last, we got on as we always do.

I still think about that morning. Not the delay. The patience.

This morning I left at my usual time. The others were already there. Nobody spoke. We all looked down the track.
