Here is what you need.

A needle. Not a sewing needle. A darning needle, with a blunt tip and a long eye. [detail needed, the size used]. Yarn to match. Close in colour and close in weight. If the yarn is too thick, the patch sits proud. You will feel it. And something round to stretch the sock over. It holds the fabric firm while you work. A spent light bulb does the job. Round and smooth, and about the size of a heel.

That is all.

Then the hole. The mistake is to mend only the hole. The hole is not the problem. It is the end of the problem. Around it the wool has gone thin. Hold it up to the light and you can see it. A ring of weak wool. Mend just the hole and the patch pulls at the thin edge. A new hole opens beside it. [fact needed, a time this happened]. So mend the whole weak patch. Not just the gap.

First the outline. I pull the sock over the bulb. I run a line of small stitches around the worn area. Well out into good wool. It marks the edge. It also gives the patch something firm to hold.

Then the long threads. I lay them lengthwise, from one side of the outline to the other. Up the sock, not across. Each one in line with the last. Close together. No gaps. Over the hole and over the thin wool around it. [detail needed, how close the threads sit].

That is the frame. The weave comes after. Across. But that is another evening.

Tonight the sock is still on the bulb. The long threads are in. The needle is stuck in the arm of the chair.
