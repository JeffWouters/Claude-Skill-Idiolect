Somebody wants a present.  It is for a friend, or an aunt, or a young man who is going away.  You are to find it.  You bring out a novel.  No, he has that--or, if he has not, he would not care for it.  You try a volume of essays.  Oh, he does not read essays.  Poems?  Poems, the customer says, are hardly the thing.  Something in a handsome binding?  Too showy.  Something plain?  Too plain.  A book of travel?  He has been everywhere.  And so on, down the length of the store and back, until the customer, looking at you with a kind of mild reproach, as though you had failed her personally, says that she will "think about it," and goes out to think about it somewhere else.

This, you might suppose, is the worst of it.  It is not.  The real difficulty of the bookseller's trade is the jump.  At one moment you are called upon to discuss, with a grave elderly gentleman, the relative merits of two editions of an ancient author, the one learned and the other more learned, and you had better know which is which.  At the next moment a young woman wishes to know whether you have "that book with the red cover that everybody is reading."  You must go from the one to the other without so much as a change of face.  You must be, within the space of a minute, a scholar and a guesser of riddles.

And the requests.  I set down some of them, as they come to mind, in no particular order.  A book on the care of the baby.  A book on the care of the dog.  A book to teach a parrot to talk.  A book "on etiquette, but not too strict."  A book of recitations suitable for a church social.  A book of jokes suitable for after-dinner.  A dream book.  A cook book.  A book "about the war," no war specified.  A book of [example needed].  A book that "will be good for a boy."  A book that a nephew, who is clever, has not read.  A prayer book, a small one, to go in a pocket.  A prayer book, a large one, to go on a table.

All of these are bookselling.  All of these, indeed, are literature.  The customer is quite sure of it.  And so, after a while, are you.
