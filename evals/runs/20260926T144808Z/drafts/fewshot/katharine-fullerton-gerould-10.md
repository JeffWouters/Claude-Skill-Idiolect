We are constantly being told that plain living and high thinking go together--that the man who does without comforts will, by some mysterious compensation, think loftier thoughts than the man who has them. I do not believe it, and I do not think the evidence supports it.

Thoreau is always brought forward as the great example. But Thoreau, if one looks at him honestly, was a good deal fonder of his comforts than his admirers like to admit. [example needed] As for Shakespeare, who thought more loftily than any of us, there is no sign that he despised an easy chair or a good dinner; the signs, such as they are, point quite the other way. [example needed] Great minds, as a rule, have liked their comforts. They have not thought the better for being cold.

Let me not be misunderstood. If we must do our own chores, we must; there is no disgrace in it, and a good deal of honest satisfaction. I have nothing to say against the woman who sweeps her own floor because there is no one else to sweep it. What I object to is the notion that she is, by that very act, more civilised than the woman who does not. She is not. She is simply busier. Civilisation has never consisted in everybody doing everything; it has consisted, on the contrary, in people doing the things they are fitted for, and leaving the rest to others who are better fitted.

And there is a further loss, which the enthusiasts for plain living seem never to have counted. Domestic service, at its best, was a craft. It had its traditions--of cookery, of housekeeping, of the care of linen and silver and furniture--handed down from one generation to the next, and kept alive by people who took a pride in them. Abolish domestic service, and you abolish those traditions. They will not survive in the hands of amateurs; they cannot. You will get, in their place, a nation of people who can all do a little of everything, and none of whom can do anything well.

That, I suppose, is what is meant by making everybody a generalist. It sounds very fine. It sounds democratic, and self-reliant, and brave. But the generalist, when you come to look at him closely, is very often merely the man who is incompetent at a great many things instead of at one; and I cannot see that the world is richer for him.
