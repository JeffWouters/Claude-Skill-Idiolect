The list lives on the fridge. One sheet of paper, held up by a magnet. That is the whole system.

It is split into three parts. Groceries at the top. Quick chores in the middle. Larger projects at the bottom. Groceries are things we have run out of, or are about to. Quick chores are things that take a few minutes. Change the bulb on the landing. Post the letter. Wipe the shelf in the fridge. Larger projects are the ones that take a weekend, or a trip to the shop, or a phone call I keep putting off. Anyone can add to any part. Anyone can cross things off.

It works because you cannot miss it. You open the fridge a dozen times a day. The list is there every time. You see what is left. You see that nobody has bought milk. You see that the gutter job has been sitting at the bottom for weeks. It nags without saying anything.

We tried phone apps first. Several. They were clever. They synced and sent reminders. Nobody opened them. A list you have to go and look for is a list you forget. The reminders came at the wrong moment and got swiped away. The apps are still on my phone, I think. Empty.

Then a notebook. That was my idea. A proper one, kept in the kitchen drawer. It lasted [duration needed]. The drawer was the problem. Out of sight, out of mind. And the notebook wandered. It ended up in a bag, or under the post, or in the car.

Then a whiteboard. That was better. It was on the wall and it was big. But the pens dried out. Then someone wiped half of it by leaning on it. Then we stopped trusting it. We took it down.

So, paper. Cheap, plain, and always in front of us. When a sheet is full I start a new one. It is the dullest system in the house. It is the only one that has lasted.
