It is worth asking why the social white lie should fall, as it undoubtedly does, so largely to women. I do not think it is because women are less truthful than men. I think it is simply because women have the social business of the world to do.

Consider the married pair. Who answers the invitations? Who writes the notes of regret, and the notes of thanks, and the notes that explain why one cannot possibly come on Thursday? The wife, of course. The husband, as a rule, does not even know that the invitation has arrived. He goes where he is taken, and he stays away when he is told; and the whole delicate apparatus of excuse and evasion is managed for him, without his knowledge, by the woman at his side. If a fib is needed--and a fib very often is needed--it is she who must tell it.

Men, moreover, are allowed a bluntness among themselves which women seldom permit to one another. A man may tell another man that he is talking nonsense, and they will dine together the next evening quite amicably. Women do not, as a rule, drop their public manner even in private. They are always, in a sense, on duty; and a person on duty cannot afford to say exactly what she thinks.

But there is another reason, and it is not a pleasant one. Women are suspected. If a woman declines to praise another woman's frock, or her house, or her husband, she is at once accused (it is always the accusation of jealousy!) of envy. Her honesty is never taken for honesty; it is taken for spite. And so, very naturally, she learns to say that the frock is charming and the house delightful--not because she is a hypocrite, but because the truth, in her mouth, would be misread. I do not blame her. I am only sorry that the world should have made the small fib so much safer than the plain word.
