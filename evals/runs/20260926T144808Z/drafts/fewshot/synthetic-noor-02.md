I leave the house at the same time every morning. Not roughly the same time. The same time. I have worked it out over years. Enough to walk to the station without hurrying. Enough to stand on the platform for a minute or two before the train comes in. No more than that. A long wait on a cold platform is wasted. A short one is a cushion.

The buffer matters. Once I left it too thin. A shoelace, a lost key, I forget which. I ran the last stretch and caught the train with the doors closing. I sat down with my heart going and my shirt damp. I did not enjoy the journey. So now I keep the buffer. Small, but there.

The same people get on with me. I know them all by sight. The man with the folded paper. The woman who always stands though there are seats. The student with the headphones and the big bag. I would find it strange now if one of them said good morning.

Then one morning the train stopped. Not at a station. In the middle of nowhere, between fields. The driver said there was a problem ahead. Then he said nothing for a long time. We sat. The heating ticked. Someone's phone buzzed and was ignored.

I expected people to give up. To ring for lifts. To get off at the next stop, when we reached it, and find another way. Nobody did. We all stayed. We waited, quietly, for [duration needed], the way we wait on the platform every day. Nobody complained out loud. When the train moved again there was no cheer. Just a small shift in the carriage. Papers opened. Heads went back down.

I got to work late. I did not mind much.
