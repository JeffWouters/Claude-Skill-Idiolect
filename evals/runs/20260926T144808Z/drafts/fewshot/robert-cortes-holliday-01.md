He wished, it appeared, to send a book.  That was all.  But it is one thing to wish to send a book and quite another thing to send it, as anybody who has stood behind a counter can tell you.  The great man approached us with the air of one who has come to the right person at last.  In his view, we gathered, we were the firm.  We took the order, we wrapped the parcel, we kept the books, we carried the thing through the streets, and very likely we had written it.  He addressed us upon each of these departments in turn, earnestly, patiently, a little anxiously, as a man speaks to a general manager.  We did not undeceive him.  One does not correct [novelist] upon the organisation of a retail business.

The book was chosen easily enough.  It was when we came to the name of the person to whom it was to go that the difficulty began.  "It is for--" he said, and stopped.  He looked at the ceiling.  He looked at us, as though we might know.  He produced a name, withdrew it, and produced another, very like the first but spelled, he thought, rather differently.  We wrote it down, both ways.  Then there was the address.  He was sure of the town.  He was fairly sure of the street.  The number of the house had gone wherever such numbers go.  "You will find it," he said, with great confidence, "I am quite certain you will find it."  We said we were quite certain, too.  It is a remarkable thing, how much of the great world rests upon the belief that somebody in a shop will find it.

Here, now, was a man who could carry in his head the whole of a family through three generations, their motives, their silences, the exact shade of their hesitations over a teacup; and he could not carry the number of a house.  Just so.  Perhaps there is only so much room.

When at last it was all written down, more or less, he drew himself up, and a look of deep relief came over his face, the look of a man who has been to the dentist and found that it was only a cleaning.  He thanked us, very courteously, at some length.  He bowed.  He went out into the street lightly, an errand done, a burden off his mind; and on to ours.
