Every autumn the pipes start to talk. The heating comes on for the first time in months and the whole house knocks and gurgles. A trickling sound in the walls. A tick in the radiator under the window. That is air. It has crept into the system over the summer and now it is in the way.

So once a year I bleed the radiators. It is a small job. It is also easy to do badly.

First find out which ones need it. Put the heating on and let it warm up. Then go round and feel each radiator. Run your hand along the top, then lower down. A radiator full of water is warm all over. A radiator with air in it is warm at the bottom and cool at the top. Sometimes the top strip is stone cold. That is the one you want. Make a note. I go round with a scrap of paper. [example needed]

Then switch the system off. This matters. If the pump is running it pushes water round and can pull more air in. It can also push hot water out of the valve at you. Let it cool down a while. Hot radiators and bare hands are a bad mix.

You need a radiator key. A small brass one. Keep it somewhere you will find it next year. I did not, once, and spent an evening looking. And a cloth. And something to catch drips.

Start on the ground floor. Work upward. Air rises, so the upper rooms are where it collects last. If you start at the top you only have to go back and do it again.

At each radiator the valve is at one end, at the top. Fit the key. Hold the cloth under it. Then turn it slowly. Just a little. You will hear a hiss. That is the air coming out. Do not open it wide. It does not come out any faster and you risk losing the little screw. Just let it hiss.
