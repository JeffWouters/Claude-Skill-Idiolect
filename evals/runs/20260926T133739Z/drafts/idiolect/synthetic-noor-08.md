Our to-do list is a sheet of paper on the fridge. It is not clever. It works.

I tried the others first. Every one of them. An app on the phone. A notebook. A whiteboard. [example needed: how each one failed]. I was sure each time that this was the one. It was not.

So back to paper. One sheet, on the fridge door.

Here is the layout. Three parts. Groceries at the top. Quick jobs in the middle. Big projects at the bottom.

The groceries are easy. You run out of something, you write it down. Whoever shops takes the list along.

The quick jobs are the ones that take no time and never get done. [example needed: a quick job]. On paper they stare at you. You open the fridge, and there they are. More of them get done. Not all.

The big projects sit at the bottom. They move slowly. Some sit there a long time. That is fine. They are there so we do not forget them, not so we feel bad about them.

Why does it beat the phone? Because you can see it. That is the whole of it. An app is out of sight until you open it, and you do not open it. A notebook is closed. A whiteboard is visible, but it wipes clean, and so do the jobs. The fridge is the one place in the house everyone goes to every day. Put the list where people already look. Then they read it.

And then the pencil. [example needed: what else I wrote with first]. A pencil rubs out, and it does not run dry. Pencil on paper has never let us down. Not once.

Tonight the sheet is half full. The pencil sits on top of the fridge.
