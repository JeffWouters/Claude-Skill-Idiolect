I finished the heel last night. It took longer than I meant it to.

The long threads were already in. Straight lines across the hole, fixed in the good wool on each side. So what was left was the weaving. This is the part that turns a hole into cloth.

Here is how it goes. Take the needle across the long threads. Over one, under the next. Then back the other way. Under where you went over, over where you went under. Row after row. Each cross thread sits close to the last.

The mistake is to pull. You want a firm patch, so you pull the thread snug. Do not. A taut thread puckers the patch, and a puckered patch rubs. You will feel it. So leave each row a little slack. Slacker than you think. The wool will settle when you wear it.

I pulled too hard on my first darns. Every one of them. I still catch myself doing it.

And it is slow. There is no way round that. [number needed] hours a heel. But the time does not feel wasted. It feels restful. The needle goes over and under. Your hands know the pattern after a few rows. Your head goes quiet. I do not get that from much else.

So why bother? Socks are cheap. But a darned heel lasts longer than the one it mends. That is the test. If the darn outlasts the sock around it, the darn was worth doing.

I learned this from a tool, not a person. It had belonged to a grandparent. [example needed: which tool]. Nobody showed me how to use it. I taught myself from that and a lot of bad first tries.

It does not last forever. In the end the ankles wear through. Thin, then gone. You can darn a heel. You cannot darn a whole sock.

This morning the pair is folded on the chair. Two good heels. Ankles still holding.
