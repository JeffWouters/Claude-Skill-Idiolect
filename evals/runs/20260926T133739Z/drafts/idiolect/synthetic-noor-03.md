I keep every chore list we have ever made. All of them. In a box.

It started by accident. A list goes up in the kitchen. Jobs get done and I strike them out. One line through each. Not a scribble. A line, so you can still read what it was. When the sheet is full, it comes down. And I could never throw a full one away. So it went in a drawer. Then the drawer filled up. Then the box.

Here is the count. [number needed] sheets. Over [number needed] years.

I do not read them often. But when I do, they tell me things I did not know about us.

First the jobs that come back. The same task, struck out, then there again a few weeks later. [example needed: a job that keeps coming back]. You do a job, and it does not stay done. That is most of a house.

Then the jobs that move. Written on one sheet, not struck out. Copied onto the next. And the next. Some of them crossed sheet after sheet before anyone did them. Some never got done at all. They stop turning up. Nobody decided that.

And then the jobs we forgot. They were on one list only. Never struck out. Never copied. Gone.

But the grocery lines are the best part. They are a record of the family. The early sheets are short. Then there is more of everything as the children grow. Longer lists. Bigger packs. And then, sheet by sheet, less again. Smaller packs. Fewer lines. One by one, they left home. You can see it in the shopping.

Tonight there is a new sheet in the kitchen. A few lines so far. None struck out yet.
