I darn my own socks. The woollen ones. It is slow work. I do not mind.

A good wool sock is worth saving. It wears thin long before it wears out. A hole starts small. Leave it and it grows. So I mend them as soon as I see one.

Here is the kit. A long needle with a big eye. Yarn that matches the sock, or near enough. And something round to stretch the sock over. There are proper tools for this. An old light bulb works as well. The glass is smooth, and the curve fits a heel.

First the sock goes over the bulb. Hole on top, the knit stretched flat but not tight. Then look at the hole. Not only the hole. The wool around it. Hold it to the light. You will see the thin part, where the knit has worn to a few strands. It is bigger than you think. Bigger than the hole itself.

That thin part is the real job. If you darn only the hole, the edge of your patch sits on weak wool. It tears there soon enough. Then you have a new hole, bigger than the old one. I learned that on my first pair. [example needed: what happened to it].

So I mark out the patch around all of it. Hole and thin wool together. An outline, a little past the worn area on every side. I run a line of small stitches round the edge to show where it is.

Then the long threads. These run lengthwise, from heel toward toe. Straight, side by side, close together. Each one starts in sound wool on one side of the outline and ends in sound wool on the other. Across the hole they cross open air. That is fine. They are the frame.

Tonight the sock sits on the bulb. The long threads are in. The cross threads are for tomorrow.
