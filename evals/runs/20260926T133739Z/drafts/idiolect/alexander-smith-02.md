We are accustomed to think of intensity as a noisy virtue. The intense poet, in the common imagination, is the poet who raves and gesticulates, who tears his passion to tatters, who cannot mention a sunset without setting the heavens on fire. When, therefore, a critic declared that the peculiar and distinguishing quality of Chaucer is intensity, many readers must have smiled; for what is there of storm and fury in that genial, observant, unhurried old story-teller? And yet the critic was right, and the smile is misplaced. Chaucer's intensity is of another kind than the one we look for. It is calm; it is direct; it is complete. It does not shout at you, and it does not need to--it simply sees, and sets down what it sees, with a concentration so entire that nothing is left over. As a clear stream shows you every pebble in its bed, and yet seems to have no depth at all until you put your arm into it, so his verse looks shallow only because it is transparent. The one kind of intensity advertises its labour; the other conceals it altogether.

That concealment is, indeed, the whole secret of the matter. Effortless art is the most laborious thing in the world. The line that reads as though it had fallen upon the page of its own accord has commonly cost its maker more thought than many of those elaborate passages which proclaim their difficulty in every syllable; and the reader, admiring the ease, never suspects the sweat. Nay, the better the work, the less it is suspected. Consider his pilgrims. You have met them; you have ridden beside them; you know the tone of their voices, the cut of their garments, the tricks of their tempers, as well as you know the habits of your neighbours. Were they less alive, we should praise the maker more. That is the ironical reward of perfection--it is mistaken for nature, and the artist is thanked by being forgotten. What is this but intensity of the highest order? A mind that could hold so many human beings steadily in view, and render each of them without a wasted stroke, was not a lazy or a lukewarm mind. It burned, but it burned without smoke!
