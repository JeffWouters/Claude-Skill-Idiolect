I leave the house at the same minute every morning. Not roughly. The same minute.

Here is the sum. [number needed] minutes to walk to the train. [number needed] minutes spare on the platform. No more. I worked it out once and have not changed it since.

The spare minutes matter. Too few and you run. Too many and you stand on a cold platform for nothing. A small margin is the point. If you are not early enough to slow down at the end, you left too late. That is the test.

Then the platform. The same people, most days. We do not speak. Nobody has ever said good morning. But I know them, and they know me. [example needed: one or two of the regulars]. And a young musician, who gets on at the same door as me. I do not know what they play. I have never asked.

When one of them is missing, I notice. I do not say anything. Nobody does. They are back the next day, or the day after.

So to the train. It is on time more often than people say. Delays come, but not often. The board changes and we all look up at once, then down again.

Once the wait was long. [number needed: how long]. The board kept changing. I thought people would give up and leave. Find a bus. Go home. Nobody did. Not one. We stood there, all of us, in our usual places. Nobody said a word about it. And when the train came in, we got on as we always do.

That is the part I think of most. Not the wait. The way nobody left.

This morning I left on the minute. The musician was at the door. We did not speak. The train was on time.
