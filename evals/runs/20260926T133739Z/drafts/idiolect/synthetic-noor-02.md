I take the same train every working day. I do not work on it any more.

I used to. Laptop open, emails answered, a head start on the day. That was the idea. Then I gave it up. Not because it failed. Because I was missing the trip. So now I watch. Or I read. I watch more than I read.

Here is what the window gives you. It turns with the year.

In winter it is dark both ways. You see your own face in the glass and the lights of each stop. Little else. In spring the light comes back first in the mornings. Then in the evenings. The fields change colour week by week. In summer it is all green and all the same. Or it looks the same. In autumn the view opens up again as the leaves drop. You see further than you think. Farms, back gardens, a road you never knew was there.

The evenings are not like the mornings. They are full. People stand in the aisles. Bags, coats, elbows. Harder to see out. Harder to read. So at night I just listen. Or I look past the shoulder in front of me at whatever strip of window is left.

And this is the odd part. The routine does not dull anything. It sharpens it. When you see the same view every day, you notice the smallest change. A new fence. A shed painted a different colour. A tree gone from a hedge. [example needed: one change I saw]. If I had worked all the way, I would have missed all of it. I am sure of that.

I did not expect this. I expected to be bored. I was not.

This morning the coach was quiet. The window was clean for once. And the view was almost the same as yesterday.
