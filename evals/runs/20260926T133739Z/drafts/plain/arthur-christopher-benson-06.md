I have never been able to believe in a God who needs to be appeased. The old idea of sacrifice, of offering something up so that an angry power will be soothed and turn its anger elsewhere, seems to me to have very little to do with what faith really is. It treats God as a sort of powerful and touchy neighbour who must be kept sweet, and it treats religion as a bargain. I cannot find either of those things at the heart of the matter.

Yet I do not think sacrifice should be thrown out altogether. It only needs to be understood rightly. True sacrifice, as I see it, is not the giving of gifts to God but the giving up of ourselves, or at least of the parts of ourselves that stand in the way. It means letting go of selfishness, of ease, of the comforts we cling to, for the sake of something worth more. That is a real cost, and often a hard one, but it is paid for a worthy end, not to buy off displeasure.

Faith, then, becomes something inward. It is not a set of transactions but a slow aligning of one's own will with God's. That is easy to say and difficult to do, because our wills are stubborn and we like our own way. It also means accepting pain when it comes, not with resentment and not with a sense that we are being punished, but trustingly, in the belief that it is not meaningless and that we are not abandoned in it. I do not pretend this is simple. But it seems to me the only kind of faith that could hold up in a real life.

And if that is faith, then worship need not be confined to any particular form. Anything that brings us to thankfulness, to calm and to humility is worship in the truest sense. It might be a service in a church, but it need not be. [example needed] What matters is the state it leaves us in, not the setting in which it happens.
