When I was a child, the past was enormous. A hundred years ago was not a number; it was a country so far away that people there must have been a different kind of creature. I suspect most children feel this, and I suspect they largely invent it. Nobody teaches a child that history is vast. The child supplies that feeling from within, because a single year is already so long for them that anything measured in centuries seems to sink into a fog of great age.

Growing up quietly undoes this. As an adult I measure time by years that feel much shorter than they used to. A year now passes like a season did at eight. And once I measure history with that shorter yardstick, the past shrinks alarmingly. A century is only a few lifetimes of the kind I am now living through at speed. Events I once thought of as ancient turn out to be almost within reach, separated from me by a chain of grandparents rather than by an abyss. The sudden closeness of history is not a discovery about history. It is a discovery about how I now feel a year.

There is a second, subtler loss. When I was young, I believed that the world of the past was fundamentally strange, full of changes that must have been dramatic and mysterious to those who lived through them. Then I lived through changes myself. I watched ordinary things appear and disappear, habits shift, whole ways of doing things become old-fashioned. None of it felt remarkable at the time. It simply happened, a little each day, and I adjusted without noticing. Change seen firsthand is almost always ordinary.

Having learned that, I cannot help applying it backwards. The people of earlier centuries presumably felt their own changes in the same flat, daily way. So the mystery drains out of the past as well as the distance. What once looked like a sequence of grand transformations now looks like a long series of ordinary days, each one no stranger to those living it than my days are to me.

I am not sure this is wisdom. It may only be a narrowing, the adult mind trading a child's sense of wonder for a more accurate but smaller picture. Either way, it is hard to get the old feeling back.
