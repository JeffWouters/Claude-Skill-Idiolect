The hiss stops. Then a spit. Then water. That is the signal. As soon as you see water, close the valve. Not before. Not long after.

Close it gently. Turn the key back until it stops. Then stop. Do not force it. The valve is small, and often old. Lean on the key and you can damage it, and then it weeps. Snug is enough. Wipe the drip with the cloth. Check it has stopped. Then the next radiator.

Ground floor first, then up. Same routine each time. Key on, turn gently, hiss, water, close.

When they are all done, go to the boiler. Look at the pressure gauge. It will have dropped. [reading needed]. The air you let out was taking up room in the system. Now it is gone, and the pressure is lower than it should be.

Then walk round again. Same as before. Hand on each radiator. Top and bottom. This time they are warm all over. Evenly. No cold band at the top. That is what you wanted.

It does not last for ever. Air gets back in. It always does. By next year there will be a cold top somewhere again. So the keys go back in their hiding places. All of them. Next year I will need to find at least one.
