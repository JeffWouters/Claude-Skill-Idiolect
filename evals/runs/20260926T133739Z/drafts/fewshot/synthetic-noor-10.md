You need three things. A long needle. Yarn that matches the sock. And something round to stretch the sock over.

The needle should be long. Long enough to reach across the hole and into some of the good wool either side. [detail needed].

The yarn should match. The colour, yes, but more than that. The thickness. [example needed]. A thread much thinner than the sock will not fill the gap. A much thicker one makes a lump you feel in your shoe.

For the round thing, I use an old light bulb. It is the right shape. It fits inside the heel and holds the wool smooth. [detail needed]. Nothing to buy.

Put the bulb in the sock so the hole sits on top of it. Now look closely. Not at the hole. Around it.

The hole is not the whole problem. The wool around it has worn thin too. Hold it up to the light and you can see it. That is where the next hole is coming from. If you only fill the hole, you join new wool to weak wool. It pulls away. So the repair has to cover the thin part as well. Go out until the wool feels firm again.

Then mark it out. I run a line of small stitches round the area. A rough box, a little bigger than the thin patch. [detail needed]. That is your outline. Everything happens inside it.

Now the lengthwise threads. Start at one corner of the outline. Take a small stitch in the good wool. Then carry the yarn across to the other side. Small stitch. Turn. Back again. Each thread next to the last, parallel. Close together, not overlapping. Do not pull them tight. Over the hole they just lie on top of the bulb.

When the box is full of parallel lines, stop. That is half the job. The other half goes crosswise.
