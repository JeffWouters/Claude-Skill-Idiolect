## The passage (by the author)

There is a list on my fridge. There has been a list on my fridge for 15 years. Not the same list. But always one.

It is held up by a magnet shaped like a lemon. The paper is A5, cut from an old pad. I write in pencil. Pencil can be rubbed out. And pencil does not run when the fridge door drips.

The list has three parts. I drew the lines with a ruler.

The top part is food. What we are out of. Milk, eggs, bread, the usual. Anyone in the house can add to it. The rule is: if you finish it, you write it.

The middle part is jobs. Small ones. Things that take less than an hour. Fix the loose hinge on the bathroom cupboard. Change the bulb on the stairs. Oil the back gate. Clean the filter in the washing machine.

The bottom part is big jobs. Things that need a day, or money, or help. Paint the hall. Clear the loft. Get the chimney swept.

So that is the system. It is not clever. But it works, and I have tried a lot of systems.

I have tried apps. Three of them. Each one worked for a month. Then I stopped opening it. An app is out of sight. A list on the fridge is not. You see it every time you want a drink of water. Eight or ten times a day. It nags you without saying a word.

I have tried a notebook. Better. But the notebook lived in a drawer. Same problem.

I have tried a whiteboard. It was good for a year. Then the pens dried out. And someone wiped it by leaning on it. I lost a whole week of jobs to the back of a coat.

So now it is paper and pencil.
