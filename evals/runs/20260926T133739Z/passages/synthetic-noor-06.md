It came loose on the third try.

And there was the washer. Black rubber, once. Now it was grey and hard and split on one edge like a dry lip. No wonder. It could not seal against anything.

I took it to the shop on the corner. The man there has a drawer of washers sorted by size. He held mine up to the light. Then he picked one out without looking. Half inch. 30 cents. I bought three.

At home the new washer went on with a small nut. I tightened it by hand, then a quarter turn with pliers. Not more. Rubber needs room to press.

Then back in with the headgear. Snug, then a little more. I wiped the threads first. Grit in the threads is how you strip them.

Handle on. Screw in. Blue cap pressed home with my thumb.

So then the valve under the sink. I turned it slowly. You want the pipes to fill without a bang. There was a hiss. Then a knock somewhere in the wall. Then quiet.

I opened the cold tap. It spat twice and ran clean. I closed it.

And I waited.

I stood at the sink and watched the spout for a full minute. Nothing. I went and made a coffee and came back. Nothing.

The whole job took 40 minutes. Most of it was the stop valve and the walk to the shop. The actual work was ten minutes. That is how it goes with small repairs. The work is short. The getting ready is long.

I put the two spare washers in a jar marked TAP. The jar lives in the drawer by the back door. It holds the old one too. I keep the old one so I remember the size.

That night the kitchen was very quiet. I noticed it when I switched off the light. No tick. No small wet sound in the dark.
