Every October the heating comes back on. And every October, the radiators knock.

It starts in the night. A tick, then a knock, then a long gurgle like a drain. The one in the back bedroom is the worst. It sounds like someone walking in the wall.

So on the first cold Saturday I bleed them. All nine. It takes about an hour.

The tool is a small brass key. It costs a few euros and it is the one tool I always lose. I keep three now. One on a hook in the boiler cupboard. One in the kitchen drawer. One in the toolbox. I can usually find one of the three.

First I turn the heating on and let it run for 20 minutes. The radiators get hot. Then I feel each one with my hand. Flat palm, top to bottom. A radiator with air in it is hot at the bottom and cold at the top. You can feel the line where the heat stops. On the back bedroom one, the line is halfway down.

Then I turn the heating off. This is the step people skip. Bleed a radiator with the pump running and you pull in more air. Or you get a jet of hot black water in the face. I have had both.

I wait ten minutes for things to settle. I make a coffee.

So then I start at the bottom of the house and work up. Downstairs first, then the landing, then the bedrooms. Air rises. If you start at the top, the air from below comes up after you.

At each radiator, the same steps.

Put an old cloth under the valve. The valve is a small square nut at one top corner. Fit the key. Hold a second cloth round the key. Then turn it a quarter turn anticlockwise. Slowly.

There is a hiss. Air coming out. It smells of metal and old water. The hiss can go on for a while. On the back bedroom one it went on for almost a minute.

And then the sound changes. The hiss goes wet. A spit. Then a bead of water. That is the moment. Turn the key back, clockwise, snug. Not tight. You can strip the little valve if you force it.

Wipe the water. Check the cloth. Move on.

Eight of the nine had some air. The one in the hall had none. It never does. I do not know why. It is the lowest one in the house and the closest to the boiler. Something to do with that.

When they are all done, I go to the boiler. There is a pressure gauge on the front. A round dial with a needle. It should sit between 1 and 1.5 bar when cold. After bleeding it drops, because you let water out. This year it was down to 0.6.

So I top it up. Under the boiler there is a short silver hose with two taps. The filling loop. I open both taps, slowly, and watch the needle climb. At 1.2 I close them. The pipes groan while the new water finds its way round.

Then the heating goes back on.

I walk round the house and feel each radiator again. Top to bottom. Hot all over. The back bedroom one too. It is the best part of the job. You put your hand flat on the metal and the heat is even from edge to edge.

That night the house was quiet. No knocks. No tick. Just the low hum of the pump in the cupboard and the click as the thermostat turned it off.

I will do the same next October. The air comes back. It always does. Small bubbles, a little at a time, over the year.

The three brass keys are back in their places now. I checked. Hook, drawer, box. The cloth is on the line outside, stained a rusty brown in nine small rings.
