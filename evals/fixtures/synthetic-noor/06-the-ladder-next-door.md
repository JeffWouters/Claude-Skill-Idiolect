My neighbour Joss owns a ladder. I do not. This has shaped our friendship more than anything else.

It is a good ladder. Aluminium, three sections, 7.5 metres at full stretch. It lives on two hooks along his side wall, under a strip of roof felt. He bought it the year he moved in. I moved in two years later.

The first time I asked for it was for the gutter. It was full of moss and a tennis ball. Water ran down the front wall every time it rained. So I knocked on his door. He came out, looked up at my gutter, and nodded. He did not say much. He just lifted the ladder off the hooks and carried it round.

And he held the bottom while I climbed. I had not asked him to. He just did it.

That was eight years ago. Since then I have borrowed the ladder, by my count, 23 times. Gutters mostly. Twice a year. The top window once, when the latch broke. The roof tiles after the storm. A wasp nest under the eaves. A kite in the tree.

He has borrowed things from me too. It is not all one way. He has had my drill, my long clamps, my wallpaper table and my car, twice. He took my lawnmower for a whole summer once, when his broke. He brought it back with the blade sharp and the box clean.

There are rules. Nobody made them. We just both know them.

You bring it back the same day. You bring it back clean. If you break something, you say so, and you fix it or replace it. You do not lend it on to a third person.

And you offer a thing back. Not money. Never money. Something else. A bag of apples from my tree. A jar of the chutney I make. When he did his loft, I went round with my drill and my second pair of hands for a Saturday.

It works. I have thought about buying my own ladder. Many times. A good one costs about 200 euros. I have the money. I have the space, just, along the back fence.

But I do not buy one. And I have worked out why.

If I owned a ladder, I would not knock on his door twice a year. He would not come out and look up at my gutter and nod. He would not hold the bottom. We would wave over the fence and that would be all.

The ladder is a reason. A small, practical reason to see each other. We are both people who need a reason.

Last spring he got ill. Nothing too bad. But he was in bed for three weeks and could not do much. His gutter filled up. I saw water running down his front wall.

So I went round and knocked. His daughter answered. I asked for the ladder. She looked puzzled. Then she went and asked him. She came back and said, go on then.

I did his gutter. Moss, leaves, a dead bird, sadly. Then I did the drain at the bottom. Then I hosed the whole run through.

I put the ladder back on its hooks. I tucked the felt over it. I left a jar of soup on his step.

A week later he was up and about. He came round with a bag of screws he had sorted out of an old tin. Sizes I needed. He did not say what it was for. I did not ask.

Last week the wind brought down a branch onto my shed roof. It was too high to reach from the ground. So I knocked on his door again. He came out and looked up and nodded.

Then he walked to his side wall. And he lifted the ladder off its two hooks, the felt sliding off onto the gravel.
