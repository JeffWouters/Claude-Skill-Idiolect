First: watch the clouds, not the sky. A blue sky tells you nothing. The clouds tell you what is coming. Thin high streaks, like combed hair, mean a change in a day or so. A grey sheet that thickens from the west means rain by evening. Tall white heaps with flat dark bases mean showers, and soon.

Second: watch the wind. Where I live, most weather comes from the west. A wind that swings from south to west means rain is on its way. A wind that swings to the north means cold and clear.

Third: watch the birds. When the swallows fly low, over the grass, rain is close. The insects they eat come down in damp air. When the swallows are high, you are fine for a while.

So that is my kit. Clouds, wind, birds. And the phone, as a check.

It matters because I do a lot outside. The garden. The allotment. Paint on the fence. Washing on the line. I ride my bike to the shops. I get it wrong less than I used to. But I still get it wrong.

Last month I hung out a full load of sheets at nine. The sky was clear. The phone said ten percent. By eleven there was a low grey band in the west. By half past, the first drops. I ran out and pulled everything in. Damp, not wet. A near thing.

I had missed the signs. There was a thin milky haze at nine, high up. And a ring round the sun. My grandmother would have seen it. She used to say a ring round the sun means rain within a day. She was not always right. But she was right that time.
