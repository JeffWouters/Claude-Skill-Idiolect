The harshest accusations made against him are that he is a boastful materialist who is impressed by sheer size, that he has disregarded older values, and that he worships money. His manners give these accusations some plausibility, but the people who make them have clearly not grasped his underlying character. Lowell called him "the Western Goth." At one time the Goths were notorious for carelessly destroying ancient art. Once they had settled down, though, these Germanic tribes proved they could create impressive things of their own, and the word "Gothic" has long since stopped being an insult. Archaeologists now even concede that the Goths did less damage to the ancient world than was originally believed. The people who did the most to destroy ancient Rome were the Romans themselves.

Western Americans are busy making money, and they find the work engaging enough that they enjoy talking about it. A casual observer concludes from this that the region is the center of a cult of wealth. However, making something is very different from worshiping it. Britain, for example, is said to include among its many industries the manufacture of idols. This is certainly a sin, but the British manufacturer reassures himself that he is only breaking half of the commandment: he makes the idol, but he does not bow down to it.
