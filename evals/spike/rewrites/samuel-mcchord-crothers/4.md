So that when Mary still remained below She mounted up with Christ upon the cross! But that too darkly I may not proceed, Francis and Poverty for these two lovers Take thou henceforward in my speech diffuse. Their concord and their joyous semblances The love, the wonder, and the sweet regard, They made to be the cause of holy thoughts.

Francis and Poverty make a very strange pair of lovers to modern readers. Our century's charitable efforts aim to eliminate poverty, and they appeal to people's rational self-interest to help achieve that goal. In such an age, it is hard to understand someone who embraced poverty as a blessing. As one of St. Francis's followers put it, "No man was ever as eager for wealth as he was for poverty."

We read of St. Francis talking with Brother Leo about what perfect happiness is. It does not come from knowledge, or power, or even from converting unbelievers to the faith. "Suppose we arrive at St. Mary of the Angels soaked with rain, freezing and starving, and we knock at the door. The porter asks, 'Who are you?' and we reply, 'We are your brothers.' Then he says, 'You're lying. You are two scoundrels who go around tricking people and stealing from the poor.' If he leaves us out in the cold and rain and we bear it patiently, telling ourselves, 'Perhaps the porter sees us as we really are,' then, Brother Leo, you may say that this is perfect happiness."

We also read his heartfelt prayer: "Lord Jesus, show me the ways of poverty that were so dear to you. Jesus, you chose to be poor. The favor I ask is that you grant me the privilege of poverty and enrich me with your blessing."
