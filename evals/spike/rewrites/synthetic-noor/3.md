But green tomatoes are not ripe ones.

August was cool again, with grey days and little sunshine. The fruit grew to full size but wouldn't ripen. I checked every morning and they stayed green. One showed a faint blush, then seemed to turn green again.

September arrived, and with it the first cold night, down to 2 degrees. I knew the season was almost over.

So I picked every tomato, ripe or not. It took an hour, and I filled two crates and a bowl.

The final count was 31 red cherry tomatoes, 12 yellow pear tomatoes, and 140 green ones of various sizes. The large ribbed variety produced only four fruits, and none of them ripened.

I laid the green ones in a single layer in the drawer of an old dresser in the hall, with a ripe banana in the middle. Bananas release a gas that helps tomatoes ripen. Over three weeks, about half turned red. They weren't as sweet as sun-ripened tomatoes, but they were fine in sauce.

I cooked the rest green, mostly as chutney: green tomatoes, onions, sugar, vinegar, and a few raisins, simmered on low heat for two hours until dark and thick. It made nine jars.

Next year I'll sow later, or grow them in the allotment greenhouse, or both. I've said this before.

This morning I opened the dresser drawer and found three left. Two were orange, and one was still green, with a dry brown stem and a small star of leaves on top.
