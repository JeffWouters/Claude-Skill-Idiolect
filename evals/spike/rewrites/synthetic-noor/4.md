While that's cooking, I peel the carrots and grate them using the coarse side of a box grater. Grated carrot melts into the soup and adds sweetness, while chopped carrot keeps its shape and texture. Either option works, but I prefer grating.

Next comes the ginger. I use a piece about as long as the top section of my thumb, scrape off the skin with a teaspoon, and grate it finely straight into the pot. The aroma rises immediately, sharp and warming.

After that, I add the spices: one teaspoon of cumin, half a teaspoon of turmeric, and a little chilli if I have some on hand. I stir them into the oil for about 30 seconds. Spices need heat and fat to release their flavour, and if you add them dry to water, they end up tasting dusty.

Then it's time for the lentils. I use 250 grams and rinse them in a sieve under running water until the water runs clear, which takes about a minute. The first rinse is cloudy and full of fine grit.

The lentils go into the pot with the carrots, followed by 1.2 litres of water, or stock if I have it. I've made the soup both ways, and the difference is minor.

I bring everything to a boil, then turn it down to a gentle simmer and leave the lid partly on.

Now there's a long wait of about 25 minutes. I set my kitchen timer, a white plastic egg that ticks as it counts down. I enjoy the ticking, because it reminds me that the soup is getting on with things without me.

In the meantime, I wash up, clear the counter, and set the table, with two bowls if someone's coming over and one if it's just me.
