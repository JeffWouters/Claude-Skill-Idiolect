You'll need three things: a darning needle, which is long and has a large eye; wool that is roughly the same thickness as the sock; and something rounded to keep the sock taut while you work. You can buy wooden darning mushrooms made specifically for this purpose, but I use an old, burnt-out light bulb of the large round type, which fits perfectly inside the heel.

Here is my method.

First, I turn the sock right side out and locate the hole. On my socks, the heels usually wear through first, followed by the ball of the foot and then the big toe. The damage is rarely just a single neat hole. Usually there's a hole surrounded by a wide area of thin, worn wool, and you need to darn that thin area too. Otherwise, a month later the hole will be back and bigger than before.

I put the bulb inside and stretch the sock tightly over it. Then I sew a line of small stitches around the worn area, about a centimetre out from the edge of the hole. This outlines the patch and also stops the damage from spreading further.

Next, I lay down the warp. This is a series of long stitches running in one direction, from top to bottom, across the hole. They sit side by side and close together, and each one catches a bit of the sound wool at the top and bottom. For a hole about the size of a coin, you'll need around 20 of these lines.

At this stage it looks rather like the strings of a small harp.

Then comes the weft, which is my favourite part. I work across from side to side, passing the needle over one warp thread and under the next, alternating as I go. At the end of each row I turn around and come back, this time going under the threads I went over before and over the ones I went under.
