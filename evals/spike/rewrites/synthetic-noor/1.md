First, pay attention to the clouds rather than the sky. A clear blue sky tells you very little, but the clouds show what's coming. Thin, high streaks that look like combed hair mean the weather will change within a day or so. A grey layer thickening from the west means rain by evening. Tall white clouds with flat, dark bases mean showers are coming soon.

Second, watch the wind. Where I live, most weather arrives from the west. A wind shifting from south to west usually means rain is on the way, while a shift to the north brings cold, clear weather.

Third, watch the birds. When swallows fly low over the grass, rain is close, because the insects they eat drop lower in damp air. If the swallows are high, you should be fine for a while.

Those are my tools: clouds, wind, and birds, with my phone as a backup.

This matters because I spend a lot of time outdoors, whether I'm gardening, working on the allotment, painting the fence, hanging out washing, or cycling to the shops. I get it wrong less often than I used to, but I still make mistakes.

Last month I hung out a full load of sheets at nine in the morning. The sky was clear and my phone gave a ten percent chance of rain. By eleven, a low grey band had appeared in the west, and by half past, the first drops were falling. I rushed out and brought everything in. The sheets were damp rather than soaked, so it was a close call.

I had missed the signs. At nine there was a thin, milky haze high up and a ring around the sun. My grandmother would have noticed it. She used to say a ring around the sun meant rain within a day. She wasn't always right, but she was that time.
