Biographers, however, have never followed the ways of the natural world. They refuse to let a person die out of sight. I have leafed through dozens of biographies, not to read them but to see whether I could find even one "Life" that was not, above all, an account of a death. I never have. No modern biography has taken nature's hint. Every one of them gives the final illness and death far more space than they deserve.

Revealing the details of a fatal illness is even more gratuitous than describing a death. Had the person recovered, the illness would rightly have remained private. Yet because he did not recover, it is treated as news for anyone who happens along. Which of us would allow the details of physical suffering we had endured and put behind us to be put on display and described? That is not something we would choose to share, and nobody has the right to seek attention or sympathy on our behalf. The story of our pain should not be told about us, since we would certainly never tell it ourselves.

There is only one thing that belongs even more exclusively to a person, and that is mental illness, or the dreams and delusions of a long delirium. When someone is, as people say, not himself, we should make up for that painful contradiction. He should be given as much privacy as a troubled mind can have. He should be left alone with that "not himself" and protected from an intrusion he is so poorly able to defend against that he could scarcely even have objected to it.
