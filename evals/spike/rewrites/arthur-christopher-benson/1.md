Not long ago I was out walking on my own through fields and villages. It was one of those sluggish early spring days when both body and mind feel slack and listless. The orchards were covered in white blossom, and the hedges were just turning green. I had recently gone back to work after a short and very pleasant holiday, and I felt the vague low mood that returning to work often brings to people who tend to worry. I went into a small old church whose door was open. It was full of sunlight and had been lovingly decorated with plenty of spring flowers. If I had been in good spirits, it would have seemed a lovely place, full of peace and beautiful mystery. But it said nothing to me and offered me no message. I was also weighed down by a painful worry about someone I loved, who was behaving badly and in a way unworthy of them. Then a sudden, gracious thought came to me: to place myself in God's hands, without hiding my weakness and anxiety, and without asking for the burden to be lifted, but asking only that I might bear His will completely.

The strength I was looking for came at once. The burden was not lightened, but I felt a deeper calm and a wish to carry it faithfully. Even the scent of the flowers seemed to blend with my promise like sweet incense, and the old walls seemed to whisper of patience and hope. I don't know where the peace that came over me then came from, but it did not seem to come from the meagre resources of my own troubled spirit.
