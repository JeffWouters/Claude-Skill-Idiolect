What does a team really tell a new member during their first few weeks? On paper, it's a set of documents, access requests, introduction meetings, and maybe a small first task meant to help them get to know the codebase. These things are important, and teams that skip them usually pay the price later. But I think the more important messages are informal. They come through the tone of early conversations, the way questions are answered, and the unspoken cues about what the team values and what it merely puts up with. A new team member is a bit like an anthropologist arriving in an unfamiliar culture, watching every gesture closely to work out the unwritten rules. What they learn in those first weeks may shape how they relate to the team for a long time, well after the official onboarding materials have been forgotten.

Think about what it's like to ask a question when you've just joined. You don't yet know which questions seem reasonable and which seem naive. You don't know whose time is scarce and who is happy to help. You don't know whether the answer is already documented somewhere you should have looked. Every question carries a small risk, and the newcomer has to keep deciding, without full information, whether that risk is worth it. The way the team handles those early questions largely determines how many more get asked. A patient, generous answer encourages people to keep asking. A rushed or slightly impatient one teaches them to struggle on their own, and that habit can last a long time, long after anyone would still think of them as new.
