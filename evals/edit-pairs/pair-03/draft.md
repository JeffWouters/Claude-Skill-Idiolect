I take the 7.42 train. I have taken it for six years.

Arguably, the walk to the station is 14 minutes. I have timed it, and with a heavy bag it is 16. In rain it is twelve, because I walk faster and take the short cut past the bins.

So I leave the house at 7.25, and that gives me three minutes of slack. Three minutes is the right amount. Less and I run. More and I stand on the platform in the cold.

Perhaps the platform has a shape I know by heart. The front two carriages stop by the yellow bench. The middle stop by the clock. The back ones stop past the end of the roof, where you get wet. I stand by the clock. The doors open right in front of me. Most days.

It seems that there is a crowd of regulars. We do not speak. But we know each other.

There is the man in the green coat who reads the paper folded in four. There is the woman with two phones, one in each hand. There is a boy with a cello case taller than he is. He gets off after two stops. I have never heard him play. But I think about the cello most mornings.

The train is late one day in five. I have kept count, on and off. Mostly by two or three minutes. Once by 40. That was the day of the fallen tree. We stood on the platform and watched the board change. Delayed. Delayed. Then a new time. Then delayed again.
