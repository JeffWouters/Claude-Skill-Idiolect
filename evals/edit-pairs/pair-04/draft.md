Perhaps this was a bad year for tomatoes. I grew them anyway.

I sowed the seeds in March, on the kitchen windowsill. Two trays. 24 cells each. Three kinds: a red cherry, a yellow pear and a big ribbed one for slicing. The packet for the ribbed one said 80 days to fruit. I laughed at that later.

It seems that they came up in six days. Thin green hooks pushing out of the soil. Then two round leaves each. Then the first true leaves, the ones with the ragged edge and that smell. You know the smell, and sharp and green. It gets on your fingers and stays.

So far so good.

But April was cold. Then May was colder, and most nights were 6 or 7 degrees. Some nights were 3. Tomatoes do not like anything under 10. They sulk. They stop growing and go a dull purple under the leaves.

I kept them inside far too long. By late May they were 40 centimetres tall and leaning at the glass. Thin, pale, reaching for the light. Leggy, the books call it.

So I made a choice. I planted them out on the first of June, cold or not.
