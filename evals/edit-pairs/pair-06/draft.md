In some ways, my neighbour Joss owns a ladder. I do not. This has shaped our friendship more than anything else.

Arguably, it is a good ladder. Aluminium, three sections, 7.5 metres at full stretch. It lives on two hooks along his side wall, under a strip of roof felt. He bought it the year he moved in. I moved in two years later.

Perhaps the first time I asked for it was for the gutter. It was full of moss and a tennis ball. Water ran down the front wall every time it rained. So I knocked on his door. He came out, looked up at my gutter, and nodded. He did not say much. He just lifted the ladder off the hooks and carried it round.

And he held the bottom while I climbed. I had not asked him to. He just did it.

That was eight years ago. Since then I have borrowed the ladder, by my count, 23 times. Gutters mostly. Twice a year. The top window once, when the latch broke. The roof tiles after the storm. A wasp nest under the eaves. A kite in the tree.

He has borrowed things from me too. It is not all one way. He has had my drill, my long clamps, my wallpaper table and my car, twice. He took my lawnmower for a whole summer once, when his broke. He brought it back with the blade sharp and the box clean.

There are rules. Nobody made them. We just both know them.
