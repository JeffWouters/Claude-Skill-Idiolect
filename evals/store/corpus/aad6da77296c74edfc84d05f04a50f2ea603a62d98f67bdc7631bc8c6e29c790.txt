I make lentil soup on Tuesdays. Not every Tuesday. Most of them.

It started in a cold week, three winters ago. The fridge was nearly bare. There was one onion, two carrots, half a bag of red lentils and a stub of ginger. So I made soup. And it was good. Better than it had any right to be.

Now it is a habit. Habits in the kitchen are worth keeping. They save you from standing at the open fridge at seven o'clock with no plan.

Here is how it goes.

First the onion. I cut it in half, then into thin half moons. Then I chop across them. Small pieces cook faster and melt into the soup. I use a heavy pot, 24 centimetres wide, with a thick base. Two spoons of oil. Medium heat. The onion goes in with a pinch of salt.

Then I wait. This is the part people rush. An onion needs ten minutes, not three. It should go soft and pale gold. Not brown. Brown is a different soup.

While it cooks I peel the carrots. I grate them on the coarse side of the box grater. Grated carrot vanishes into the soup and turns it sweet. Chopped carrot stays as carrot. Both are fine. I like grated.

And the ginger. A piece the size of my thumb to the first knuckle. I scrape the skin off with a teaspoon. Then I grate it fine, straight into the pot. The smell comes up at once. Sharp and warm.

Then the spices. One teaspoon of cumin. Half a teaspoon of turmeric. A little chilli if I have it. I stir them into the oil for 30 seconds. Spices need fat and heat to wake up. Dry spices in water taste of dust.

So then the lentils. 250 grams. I rinse them first in a sieve under the tap until the water runs clear. It takes about a minute. The first water is cloudy and full of fine grit.

The lentils go in with the carrots. Then 1.2 litres of water. Stock if I have it. Water if I do not. I have made it both ways and the difference is small.

Bring it up to a boil. Then down to a low bubble. Lid half on.

And now the long wait. 25 minutes. I set the kitchen timer. It is a white plastic egg that ticks. I like the tick. It tells me the soup is going on without me.

I do the washing up. I clear the counter. I lay the table. Two bowls if someone is coming. One if not.

Red lentils do a strange thing. They start as small orange stones. Then they turn yellow and fall apart. By the end there is no lentil left, only a thick gold soup. You do not need a blender. A whisk is enough. Ten strong turns and it is smooth.

Then the last part. The part that matters most.

Lemon. Half a lemon, squeezed in at the end. Then salt. More salt than you think. I taste, add a pinch, taste again. Three times, most weeks. The soup goes from flat to bright in one pinch. It is like a light coming on.

Some weeks I add a tin of tomatoes. Some weeks a handful of spinach at the end. Once I added coconut milk and it was too rich. I did not do that again.

The whole thing costs very little. The lentils are the most of it, and they are cheap. It makes four bowls. I eat one and put three in the fridge. By Thursday it is thicker and better. I thin it with a splash of water.

I have tried to write the recipe down for friends. It never quite works on paper. The numbers are right. But the onion time, the salt at the end, the lemon: those are the soup. And they are hard to write.

Tonight it was raining. I ate at the table by the window with the lamp on. Steam on the glass. A spoon resting in an empty bowl.
