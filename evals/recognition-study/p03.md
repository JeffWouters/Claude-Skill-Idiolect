The greengrocer on the corner orders two boxes. Most of them go to me and to my neighbour, who keeps bees. I buy 2 kilos. They cost 6.40 euros this year. Last year they cost 5.90.

They are not pretty oranges. The skins are thick and rough and pitted. Some have a green patch near the stalk. The smell is sharp, not sweet. You notice it on your hands for a day.

The pan was my grandmother's. It is aluminium, wide and heavy, with a dent on one side. It holds nine litres. I use it for nothing else.

Here is the method.

I scrub the oranges in cold water with a nail brush. I cut them in half and squeeze out the juice. The pips go into a square of muslin. So does the pith I scrape out with a spoon. That is where the set comes from. Without it the marmalade stays runny.

Then I cut the peel. This is the long part. I cut it thin, about 3 millimetres, on the old board by the window. It takes two hours. I listen to the radio and do not hurry. Thick peel stays chewy. Thin peel goes soft and clear.

The peel goes into the pan with the juice and 4 litres of water. The muslin bag is tied to the handle with string. It hangs down into the water like a tea bag. Then it all sits overnight.

In the morning I simmer it. Two hours, lid off, low heat. The peel is done when it squashes flat between finger and thumb. If it is still firm, the sugar will harden it for good. I learned that in the second winter. 19 jars of something like toffee with bits in.

So then I squeeze the bag. I let it cool first. I press it against the side of the pan with a wooden spoon, then with my hands. A thick grey jelly comes out. It looks like nothing. It does all the work.
