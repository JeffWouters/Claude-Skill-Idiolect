I wash the windows twice a year. Once in April, once at the end of September. There are 11 of them, not counting the small one in the shed. I do the inside and the outside on the same day. Otherwise it does not count.

The method came from my grandmother. She used newspaper and vinegar and nothing else. I have tried the blue spray from the supermarket. It leaves a film. You do not see it until the sun gets low, and then you see nothing else. So I went back to newspaper.

Here is what I use.

A plastic bucket. Warm water, about four litres. 200 millilitres of white vinegar. A soft cloth for the frames. A chamois for the sills. A stack of old newspaper, torn into half sheets. The short stepladder from the shed.

I pick a grey day. That is the first rule. Sun dries the glass before you can wipe it, and then you get streaks. Grey and still is best. A little wind is fine. Rain is not.

I start upstairs and work down. Drips run down, never up. If you do the ground floor first, you do it twice.

First the frames. I wipe the sills and the frames with the cloth and plain water. Dead flies. Old spider silk. A grit of dust in the corners. If you skip this, the dirt from the frame ends up on the glass.

So then the glass. I dip the cloth in the vinegar water and wet the whole pane. Not dripping. Just wet. Then I take a half sheet of newspaper and crumple it into a loose ball. About the size of a fist.

You wipe in circles first. Then top to bottom in straight lines. The paper squeaks when the glass is clean. It is a dry, thin sound, like a finger on a balloon. When it stops squeaking, the paper is too wet. It goes in the bag and I take a new one.

The ink does no harm. People expect grey hands and grey glass. The hands, yes. The glass comes up clear. I think the ink helps it polish. I have never proved it.

The outside is the hard part. The upstairs windows open outward on a hinge. From inside I can reach most of the outer pane. Most, not all. The bottom corner of the back bedroom I cannot reach at all. I lean out as far as is sensible and leave a strip about the width of a hand.

Three years ago I went out on the ladder for that corner. The ladder stood in the soft bed under the window. One foot sank. The whole thing leaned left. I came down slowly and put the ladder away.

That strip waits for the window cleaner. He comes once a year with a long pole and a hose. He charges 35 euros for the whole house, outside only.

Downstairs is easy. I do the outside from the path and the inside from a kitchen chair. The big window in the front room is two metres wide. It takes six sheets of newspaper on its own. I do it last, when the bucket is cold.

Hanna watched from over the fence while I did her side of the kitchen window. The side that faces her garden. Later she said, you missed a bit. She was right. A grey arc at the top of the landing window, where my arm did not go.

Here is the count from last Saturday.

11 windows. 38 half sheets of newspaper. One bucket of water, changed once. Two hours and 40 minutes, with a stop for tea at 11.15. One dead wasp on the landing sill. One grey arc.

But I could pay for all of it. The window cleaner would do the inside too, for another 20 euros. I know that. I have known it for years.

I keep doing it because of the week after. The rooms look bigger. The garden comes right up to the glass. The blackbird on the fence looks close enough to touch.

And the paper still does it better than anything I have bought.

This evening the sun came low across the back garden at about 18.40. It lit every pane in the kitchen and showed nothing at all. Upstairs, the one strip in the back bedroom. Pale and dusty along the bottom, waiting for the pole.
